Technical Analysis

18 · Hannah OhField Quality

Daeun, Arun, let's understand the mechanism. Why would a charger cause ghost touches?

19 · Arun PillaiSensira Touch Engineering

Capacitive touch sensing measures very small changes in charge on the screen. A charger, especially a low-quality one, can inject electrical noise into the device's ground. If that noise happens to be at a frequency close to the one our controller uses for sensing, the controller can mistake the noise for a finger. Our firmware has a noise detection mechanism: when it detects noise, it switches to a different sensing frequency. That's called frequency hopping.

20 · Daeun LeeTouch & Input Software

And humidity makes it worse in two ways. Moisture on the screen surface changes the baseline capacitance, and sweaty or damp fingers change the signal from real touches. So in humid conditions, the margin between noise and a real touch gets smaller, and the controller has a harder time telling them apart.

21 · Hannah OhField Quality

That explains the ghost touches. What about the unresponsive screen?

22 · Daeun LeeTouch & Input Software

That's the frequency hopping itself. When the controller decides the noise is too high, it pauses reporting, scans for a cleaner frequency, recalibrates, and resumes. With a very noisy charger, it can do this several times in a row, and each cycle takes about half a second. The user experiences that as the screen freezing for a few seconds.

23 · Arun PillaiSensira Touch Engineering

I should also say, in fairness to our firmware, that we tested with the chargers listed in your compliance plan, and the firmware handled them well. Some of the third-party chargers sold in these markets produce noise levels far outside any standard. We can't design for every charger in the world.

24 · Hannah OhField Quality

I understand that, Arun, and I don't think anyone is blaming the firmware for bad chargers. But from the customer's point of view, the phone misbehaves and other phones don't. Is that true, Mai? Do competitor phones have the same problem with the same chargers?

25 · Mai NguyenSoutheast Asia Service Operations

Our technicians did an informal test last week with three popular cheap chargers. Two competitor phones showed occasional ghost touches with one charger, but much less than Orion Lite. So it's not unique to us, but we seem more sensitive.

26 · Arun PillaiSensira Touch Engineering

That's useful data. If you can send us those chargers, we can characterize their noise in our lab. There may be firmware improvements we can make: a wider range of frequencies to hop to, and a smarter filter that can tell the difference between a noise spike and a real finger.
