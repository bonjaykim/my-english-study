Kicking Off the OS Upgrade Program for Orion and Orion Lite

Orion·Orion Lite OS 업그레이드 프로그램 킥오프

출시된 Orion과 Orion Lite를 다음 안드로이드 버전으로 업그레이드하는 프로그램의 킥오프 회의입니다. 프로그램 관리, 플랫폼 프레임워크, 커널·BSP, SQE 팀이 개발 시작일, 베타와 정식 배포 일정, Atlas와의 인력 충돌, 커널 유지 여부를 논의합니다.

Attendance and Introductions

01 · Joanne LeeProgram Management

Good morning, everyone, and thank you for coming in on a Saturday. I know several of you were in the Atlas checkpoint at ten, so I'll try not to repeat things you've already heard today. I'm Joanne from Program Management, and I'll be running the OS upgrade program for Orion and Orion Lite. Let's go around the table.

02 · Brian ShinPlatform Framework

Brian, Platform Framework. My team will do most of the heavy lifting on this one, since an OS upgrade is mostly a framework merge.

03 · Elena ParkPlatform Framework

Elena, also Framework. I'll be the technical lead for the merge itself, and I'll coordinate with the app teams on compatibility.

04 · Chris MoonKernel & BSP

Chris, Kernel and BSP. I'm here mainly to discuss whether we keep the current kernel or move to a newer one, and what that means for the vendor.

05 · Minji SeoSQE

Minji, SQE lead for Orion. And Ryan is joining me because he'll own the carrier certification plan.

06 · Ryan ChaSQE

Hi, Ryan Cha, SQE. I coordinate with the carrier labs, so most of my questions today will be about dates.

Program Background and Commercial Targets

07 · Joanne LeeProgram Management

Let me start with the background. Google's next Android release is planned for early December, according to the partner timeline Kevin shared last week. Our commitment to customers is that Orion gets the upgrade, and Orion Lite gets it as well, since both are within their support window. Last year, our competitors delivered their upgrades between four and ten weeks after Google's release. The business would like us to be at the front of that range this time.

08 · Joanne LeeProgram Management

So the headline target from the business is a stable rollout for Orion in mid-January, roughly six weeks after Google. Orion Lite would follow about a month later. I'll be honest: that target was set before anyone asked engineering, which is exactly why we're here today.

09 · Brian ShinPlatform Framework

I appreciate you saying that up front. Mid-January would be ambitious even in a normal year, and this is not a normal year, because Atlas is pulling on the same framework engineers. I'd like to understand how much flexibility there is in that date before we commit to anything.

10 · Joanne LeeProgram Management

There's some. What the business really cares about is not being the last one. If we can give them a credible plan with a clear beta milestone, I think February is negotiable. What they won't accept is a vague answer.

Scope of the Framework Merge

11 · Joanne LeeProgram Management

Elena, let's start with the biggest piece of work. How big is the framework merge, and how long will it take?

12 · Elena ParkPlatform Framework

Based on the developer preview we've already pulled, there are roughly fourteen hundred files where Google's changes overlap with our modifications. From experience, about seventy percent of those resolve mechanically. The remaining four hundred or so need someone who understands both sides. My estimate is six weeks for the merge to reach a booting, roughly functional build, and another four weeks to restore feature parity with our current Orion software.

13 · Joanne LeeProgram Management

So ten weeks of framework work before we have something we can seriously test. If we started on August 17th, that takes us to the end of October.

14 · Brian ShinPlatform Framework

That's with a full team, though. Here's the problem. Atlas framework bring-up also starts in late August, and it needs about sixty percent of the same people. If I split the team, both programs slow down. Realistically, with the people I can spare, the upgrade merge takes closer to fourteen weeks.

15 · Joanne LeeProgram Management

Fourteen weeks puts us at the end of November, before Google's final release even ships. Is that actually a problem? We'd be merging against preview builds anyway.

16 · Elena ParkPlatform Framework

It's not a problem by itself. But between the last preview and the final release, Google usually changes a few hundred files. We'd need a second, smaller merge in December. That typically takes two to three weeks, and it's the part that most often surprises people.

Kernel and Vendor Dependencies

17 · Joanne LeeProgram Management

Chris, what about the kernel side? Do we need a new kernel for the upgrade?

18 · Chris MoonKernel & BSP

Good news here, for once. Under Google's rules, devices that upgrade can keep their launch kernel branch, as long as it's still within its support period. Orion and Orion Lite are both on the 6.1 branch, which is supported well beyond next year. So we don't have to do a kernel uplift. That saves us easily two months of work and a lot of risk.

19 · Minji SeoSQE

That's a relief. But the vendor side still changes, doesn't it? The new Android version comes with new vendor interface requirements.

20 · Chris MoonKernel & BSP

Right. The kernel stays, but some hardware abstraction layers need updates to meet the new interface versions. For Orion, Qorvix has to deliver updated camera, audio, and graphics components. I asked them informally last week, and they said they could deliver a first drop by mid-October and a final one by mid-November. I'll get that in writing.

21 · Brian ShinPlatform Framework

Mid-November for the final vendor drop is fine for us, as long as the first drop in October already has the new interfaces, even if the implementations are rough. We need the interfaces to compile the framework against.

22 · Chris MoonKernel & BSP

I'll make that an explicit requirement when I talk to them. I'll also ask for their test results on the compatibility suites with each drop, because last year their first upgrade drop failed about three hundred vendor tests, and we only found out when we ran them ourselves two weeks later.

Testing and Certification Timeline

23 · Joanne LeeProgram Management

Let's talk about testing, because that's usually where upgrade schedules die. Minji, Ryan?

24 · Minji SeoSQE

From SQE's side, an OS upgrade needs three things. First, our full regression suite, which takes about three weeks per round, and we always need at least two rounds. Second, an upgrade-specific test: we take devices with real user data on the old version, upgrade them, and make sure nothing is lost and every app still works. That's the one that catches the scariest bugs. Third, Google's compatibility test suites, which have to pass before we can ship at all.

25 · Ryan ChaSQE

And then there are the carriers. Orion is sold through forty-two carriers worldwide. The big ones in North America and Korea require their own certification of an OS upgrade, which takes between four and eight weeks after we submit. Some of them also have holiday freezes: two of the US carriers don't accept new submissions between December 15th and January 5th.

26 · Joanne LeeProgram Management

That holiday freeze is a big deal. If we can't submit before December 15th, those carriers effectively push us into February, no matter how fast we are.

27 · Ryan ChaSQE

Exactly. On the other hand, unlocked devices and markets without carrier certification, like most of Europe, could go earlier, as long as Google's compatibility tests pass. So we might want a split rollout: open markets first, then carrier markets as approvals come in.

Building the Milestone Plan

28 · Joanne LeeProgram Management

Let me try to put this together into a draft plan, and you tell me where it breaks. Development starts on August 17th with a reduced framework team. Internal dogfood on preview-based software starts November 2nd. The final Google release lands in early December, and we finish the second merge by December 18th. A public beta for Orion opens on December 21st in Korea and the US. Stable rollout in open markets starts on February 1st, with carrier markets following through February.

29 · Minji SeoSQE

November 2nd for dogfood is tight, given Brian's fourteen-week estimate. You'd be giving internal users a build that isn't feature complete. I'm okay with that for engineers, but not for the broader company dogfood population.

30 · Joanne LeeProgram Management

That's a good distinction. Let's say engineering dogfood on November 2nd, and company-wide dogfood on November 30th, once feature parity is restored.

31 · Elena ParkPlatform Framework

That works for me. And for the public beta on December 21st, I'd want at least one week of company-wide dogfood on the final Google release first. December 18th to the 21st is only three days.

32 · Joanne LeeProgram Management

Then let's move the public beta to January 4th. That still gives us four weeks of beta before February 1st. Ryan, does that work with the carrier freeze?

33 · Ryan ChaSQE

Only if we submit to the US carriers before December 15th, which means submitting a build that isn't final yet. Some carriers accept a pre-final submission for early testing and then a quick delta review for the final build. I'd need to negotiate that with each of them, but it's been done before.

34 · Brian ShinPlatform Framework

I want to flag something before we lock this. Everything we just said assumes the reduced framework team stays reduced, not that it shrinks further. If Atlas hits a crisis in October and pulls more people, this plan breaks. I'd like a rule that pulling people from the upgrade requires a conversation at the program director level, not just a hallway decision.

35 · Joanne LeeProgram Management

That's very reasonable, and I'll take it to the program directors on Monday. I'll present it as a dependency between the two programs, so it's visible to both sides.

Designing the Public Beta

36 · Joanne LeeProgram Management

Let's spend a few minutes on the public beta itself, since it's the first thing customers will see. How many participants are we aiming for, and what do we actually want to learn from it?

37 · Minji SeoSQE

For a flagship upgrade, I'd aim for around twenty thousand participants across Korea and the US. That's enough to see crash patterns we'd never find in the lab, especially with third-party apps. What I want to learn most is the upgrade success rate and the top crashing apps after the upgrade, not opinions about new features.

38 · Elena ParkPlatform Framework

I'd add one more goal: app compatibility. Every Android release changes some behaviors that apps depend on. In the beta, we should automatically report any app that crashes more than twice as often as it did on the old version, and share that list with our developer relations team every week.

39 · Ryan ChaSQE

One practical point. Beta users need a way back. If someone joins the beta and their banking app stops working, they need to be able to roll back to the stable version without losing data. Otherwise, we'll have angry customers and service center visits.

40 · Elena ParkPlatform Framework

Rolling back without data loss is harder than it sounds, because the new version migrates databases to new formats. The safe approach is to back up the user data before the upgrade and restore it on rollback. It's doable, but it needs to be designed now, not in December.

41 · Joanne LeeProgram Management

Then let's make the rollback path a formal requirement for the beta. Elena, can you include it in the merge plan as its own work item, with an owner and a date?

42 · Elena ParkPlatform Framework

Yes. I'll scope it this week and give you a realistic estimate by the fourteenth.

Orion Lite and Risk Review

43 · Joanne LeeProgram Management

Now Orion Lite. The business wants it about a month after Orion. Is that realistic?

44 · Elena ParkPlatform Framework

Most of the framework work is shared, so Orion Lite mainly needs its own device-specific integration and testing. Six weeks after Orion is realistic. Four weeks would mean overlapping testing with the Orion beta, and SQE would be stretched very thin.

45 · Minji SeoSQE

I agree with Elena. Six weeks lets us reuse the same test devices and the same people. So Orion Lite stable around March 15th.

46 · Joanne LeeProgram Management

Let's list the top risks so I can put them in the plan. I have: Atlas pulling framework engineers, Qorvix vendor drops slipping, the carrier holiday freeze, and Google's final release moving. Anything else?

47 · Minji SeoSQE

One more: data migration. Every OS upgrade changes some internal database formats. If the migration fails on even a small percentage of devices, users lose data, and that's the kind of thing that ends up in the news. I want the upgrade-specific test to start as early as engineering dogfood, not after the beta.

48 · Chris MoonKernel & BSP

Agreed, and please include devices that are almost full. Storage-related migration failures almost always show up on devices with less than five percent free space.

Decisions and Action Items

49 · Joanne LeeProgram Management

Okay, let me summarize the plan. Development starts August 17th with a reduced framework team. Engineering dogfood November 2nd. Company dogfood November 30th. Second merge complete December 18th. Pre-final carrier submissions before December 15th. Public beta January 4th. Orion stable in open markets February 1st, carrier markets through February. Orion Lite stable around March 15th. Kernel stays on 6.1. Does anyone object?

50 · Brian ShinPlatform Framework

No objection, with the staffing rule we discussed.

51 · Joanne LeeProgram Management

Noted. Action items: Elena, share the detailed merge plan and the list of risky areas by August 14th. Chris, get written confirmation of the Qorvix vendor drop dates and the interface requirement by August 12th. Minji, design the upgrade-specific test, including near-full devices, by the end of August. Ryan, start negotiating pre-final submissions with the US and Korean carriers. Brian, confirm the names of the engineers assigned to the upgrade. And I'll take the staffing rule and the February date to the program directors on Monday.

52 · Ryan ChaSQE

I'll start with the two US carriers that have the holiday freeze, since they're the biggest risk to the date. If either of them refuses a pre-final submission, I'll let you know immediately, so we can decide whether to move the beta or accept a later date for those markets.

Closing and Next Meeting

53 · Joanne LeeProgram Management

Thank you, everyone. I think this is a plan I can defend, and that's what I needed. We'll meet every other Saturday at 2 p.m. Korea time while the program is ramping up. The next meeting is August 22nd, same room. By then, I'd like the merge plan, the vendor dates, and a first read from the carriers.

54 · Elena ParkPlatform Framework

Sounds good. I'll start pulling the latest preview into a branch on Monday, so we're ready to go on the seventeenth.

55 · Minji SeoSQE

Thanks, Joanne. It's a relief to start an upgrade program with realistic dates for once.

프로그램 배경과 사업 목표

배경부터 말씀드리겠습니다. 지난주 Kevin이 공유한 파트너 일정에 따르면 Google의 다음 안드로이드 릴리스는 12월 초로 계획돼 있습니다. 고객에 대한 저희 약속은 Orion과 Orion Lite 모두 지원 기간 안에 있으니 둘 다 업그레이드를 받는다는 겁니다. 작년에 경쟁사들은 Google 릴리스 후 4주에서 10주 사이에 업그레이드를 내놓았습니다. 사업부는 이번에 저희가 그 범위의 앞쪽에 있기를 원합니다.

그걸 처음부터 말씀해 주셔서 감사합니다. 1월 중순은 평범한 해에도 야심 찬 목표인데, 올해는 Atlas가 같은 프레임워크 엔지니어들을 끌어가고 있어서 평범한 해가 아닙니다. 뭔가를 약속하기 전에 그 날짜에 얼마나 유연성이 있는지 이해하고 싶습니다.

10 · Joanne LeeProgram Management

어느 정도는 있습니다. 사업부가 진짜 신경 쓰는 건 꼴찌가 되지 않는 겁니다. 명확한 베타 마일스톤이 있는 신뢰할 만한 계획을 주면, 2월은 협상 가능하다고 봅니다. 사업부가 받아들이지 않는 건 모호한 답변입니다.

프레임워크 머지 범위

11 · Joanne LeeProgram Management

Elena, 가장 큰 작업부터 시작하죠. 프레임워크 머지 규모가 얼마나 되고, 얼마나 걸릴까요?

12 · Elena ParkPlatform Framework

이미 받아 둔 개발자 프리뷰 기준으로, Google의 변경과 저희 수정이 겹치는 파일이 약 1,400개입니다. 경험상 그중 70% 정도는 기계적으로 해결됩니다. 나머지 400개 정도는 양쪽을 다 이해하는 사람이 필요합니다. 제 추정으로는 머지가 부팅되고 대략 동작하는 빌드에 도달하는 데 6주, 현재 Orion 소프트웨어와 기능 동등성을 회복하는 데 4주가 더 걸립니다.

13 · Joanne LeeProgram Management

그러니까 제대로 테스트할 수 있는 결과물이 나오기까지 프레임워크 작업만 10주군요. 8월 17일에 시작하면 10월 말이 되네요.

14 · Brian ShinPlatform Framework

그런데 그건 팀 전원이 붙었을 때 얘기입니다. 문제는 이겁니다. Atlas 프레임워크 브링업도 8월 말에 시작하는데, 같은 사람들 중 약 60%가 필요합니다. 팀을 나누면 두 프로그램 모두 느려집니다. 현실적으로 제가 내줄 수 있는 인원으로는 업그레이드 머지가 14주 가까이 걸립니다.

15 · Joanne LeeProgram Management

14주면 11월 말인데, Google 최종 릴리스가 나오기도 전이네요. 그게 실제로 문제가 되나요? 어차피 프리뷰 빌드 기준으로 머지하게 될 텐데요.

그 자체로는 문제가 아닙니다. 하지만 마지막 프리뷰와 최종 릴리스 사이에 Google은 보통 수백 개 파일을 바꿉니다. 12월에 두 번째, 더 작은 머지가 필요합니다. 보통 2~3주 걸리는데, 사람들이 가장 자주 놀라는 부분이 바로 그겁니다.

최종 벤더 드롭이 11월 중순이면 저희는 괜찮습니다. 단, 10월 첫 드롭에 구현이 엉성하더라도 새 인터페이스는 이미 들어가 있어야 합니다. 프레임워크를 그 인터페이스에 맞춰 컴파일해야 하거든요.

테스트와 인증 일정

23 · Joanne LeeProgram Management
