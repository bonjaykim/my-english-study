Responding to File System Corruption after Sudden Power Loss

갑작스러운 전원 차단 후 파일 시스템 손상 보고 대응

서비스 센터들로부터 배터리가 완전히 방전되거나 강제로 꺼진 Orion 단말이 파일 시스템 손상으로 부팅하지 못한다는 보고가 늘고 있습니다. 시장 품질, 커널·BSP, 글로벌 서비스 운영, 메모리 벤더 Kestrel이 저온에서의 배터리 잔량 오차, 저장 장치 쓰기 캐시와 종료 시 플러시 순서, 데이터 복구 방안과 수정 일정을 논의합니다.

Service centers have reported a growing number of Orion devices that can't boot after a sudden power loss and need to be reset. I want to understand why and what we can do, quickly.

What Service Centers See

07 · Hannah OhField Quality

Marcus, could you describe what's coming into the service centers?

08 · Marcus LeeGlobal Service Operations

The customer usually says the phone died, either because the battery ran out completely or because it froze and they forced it off. When they charge it and turn it on, it shows a message that the data is corrupted and offers a factory reset. Unless they have a recent backup, they lose their photos and messages. In August, we received about four hundred such devices worldwide, compared with about a hundred and twenty in May.

09 · Hannah OhField Quality

Three times higher in three months. Is there any pattern in where they come from?

10 · Marcus LeeGlobal Service Operations

Yes, and it surprised me. A disproportionate number come from cold regions in the southern hemisphere, where it's winter now: southern Australia, New Zealand, Chile, and Argentina. And many customers mention that the phone died outdoors, while the battery indicator still showed a few percent.

11 · Kyle JangField Quality

That matches the telemetry. For devices that later came in with corruption, the last reported battery level before the failure was between two and six percent in most cases, and the battery temperature was below five degrees Celsius. Normal devices that shut down from low battery report one percent and shut down cleanly.

12 · Yuna KwakKernel & BSP

So the phone thinks it has a few percent left, but in the cold, the battery can't actually deliver enough power, and the voltage collapses before the system has a chance to shut down properly.

Why a Sudden Shutdown Corrupts Data

13 · Hannah OhField Quality

But phones lose power suddenly all the time. Why does it corrupt the file system on these devices and not others?

14 · Yuna KwakKernel & BSP

Our file system is designed to survive sudden power loss. It writes data in a way that, if the power goes away at any moment, the last consistent checkpoint is still intact. But that guarantee depends on one assumption: when the storage device says a write is complete, the data is really on the flash, not just in a temporary cache.

15 · Stefan BrandtKestrel Memory

And that's where I should explain something about the firmware we released in July. It enabled a larger write buffer to improve performance. Data in that buffer is volatile until it's flushed to flash. The file system sends an explicit flush command at each checkpoint, and our firmware honors it. So under normal conditions, the guarantee holds.

16 · Hyunsoo BaeKernel & BSP

Under normal conditions. But a flush takes time, and with a larger buffer, it takes longer. I measured it this summer during the timeout investigation: a full flush can take up to six hundred milliseconds with the new firmware, compared with under two hundred before. If the voltage collapses in the middle of a checkpoint's flush, the checkpoint is half written.

17 · Stefan BrandtKestrel Memory

I should add that our storage part has power-loss protection for its own internal data, so the storage itself never becomes unusable. But it can't protect data the host hasn't finished flushing. That's a system-level guarantee, not something the storage can provide alone.

18 · Hannah OhField Quality

So we have a chain again: cold weather makes the battery estimate wrong, the device shuts down abruptly instead of gracefully, and the larger write buffer makes the window for a half-written checkpoint much wider.

19 · Yuna KwakKernel & BSP

Exactly. And in the file system, a half-written checkpoint should still fall back to the previous one. I'm checking whether there's a bug in the recovery logic, because a single bad checkpoint shouldn't require a full reset.

The Recovery Logic

20 · Hannah OhField Quality

What have you found so far?

21 · Yuna KwakKernel & BSP

Marcus's team sent me disk images from five affected devices, with customer consent. In all five, the newest checkpoint is damaged, but the previous checkpoint is intact. The file system check tool at boot detects the damage, but instead of rolling back to the previous checkpoint, it reports the volume as corrupted and gives up. That's a policy setting in our boot scripts, chosen years ago to be conservative.

22 · Marcus LeeGlobal Service Operations

Wait. So the data is actually there, and recoverable?

23 · Yuna KwakKernel & BSP

In all five images I looked at, yes. I ran the check tool manually in repair mode, and it rolled back to the previous checkpoint. The only data lost was whatever was written in the last thirty seconds before the power loss. Photos, messages, and apps were all intact.

24 · Marcus LeeGlobal Service Operations

That changes everything for us. Right now, our technicians reset these devices because that's what the screen recommends. If there's a repair mode that works, we could recover data for most of these customers instead of wiping it.

25 · Hannah OhField Quality

That's the most urgent action, then. Every day we keep resetting devices, we're deleting data that could have been saved.

Immediate Actions for Service Centers

26 · Marcus LeeGlobal Service Operations

What would my technicians need to run the repair mode safely?

27 · Yuna KwakKernel & BSP

The service tool already has a way to run commands on a device in recovery mode. I can write a script that first makes a raw backup of the data partition to the service center's computer, then runs the check tool in repair mode, then verifies the result. The backup step is important: if the repair fails for any reason, the original data is still preserved.

28 · Kyle JangField Quality

Encrypted data is a concern, though. The data partition is encrypted with keys tied to the user's lock screen. Can the repair work without the user's PIN?

29 · Yuna KwakKernel & BSP

Good question. The file system metadata that needs repair isn't encrypted, only the file contents are. So the repair doesn't need the user's PIN, and it never sees the user's files in readable form. That's important for privacy, and it means technicians can run it without asking for the customer's credentials.

30 · Marcus LeeGlobal Service Operations

Then I'd like to issue a service bulletin as soon as the script is tested: for any Orion device showing the corruption message, run the repair procedure before considering a reset. How quickly can the script be ready?

31 · Yuna KwakKernel & BSP

I can have it tested on twenty artificially corrupted devices by Monday. If you can hold any affected devices that come in until then, rather than resetting them, we can recover those customers' data too.

32 · Marcus LeeGlobal Service Operations

I'll send an instruction to all service centers today: hold these devices, offer the customer a loaner phone, and don't reset anything until Monday.

Software Fixes

33 · Hannah OhField Quality

Now let's prevent it from happening in the first place. What can we change in the device software?

34 · Yuna KwakKernel & BSP

Three things. First, change the boot policy so the check tool automatically rolls back to the previous checkpoint instead of giving up. That alone would turn almost all of these cases into a normal boot with thirty seconds of lost data. Second, make the low-battery shutdown temperature-aware: in the cold, start the shutdown at five percent instead of one, because the battery can't be trusted below that.

35 · Hyunsoo BaeKernel & BSP

Third, when the battery is critically low, we can ask the storage to flush more often, so there's less data sitting in the volatile buffer at any moment. It costs a little performance, but at five percent battery, nobody is running benchmarks.

36 · Stefan BrandtKestrel Memory

We can help with the third one. Our firmware supports a mode that limits the size of the write buffer when the host requests it. Instead of flushing more often, you can simply tell the storage to use a smaller buffer when the battery is low. It's cleaner, and the flush time drops back to under two hundred milliseconds.

37 · Hyunsoo BaeKernel & BSP

That's better than what I proposed. I didn't know that mode existed. Could you send me the command specification?

38 · Stefan BrandtKestrel Memory

Of course, today. I'd also recommend using it during the shutdown sequence in general, not only at low battery. Any shutdown benefits from a smaller buffer.

Timeline and Risk

39 · Hannah OhField Quality

Which of these can go into the October update, and which need more time?

40 · Yuna KwakKernel & BSP

The boot policy change is small, but it's sensitive: it changes what happens to user data after a crash. I want it tested very thoroughly, including on devices with real corruption from the field. The temperature-aware shutdown is simple and low risk. Both can make the October cutoff on the twenty-fifth if I start this week.

41 · Hyunsoo BaeKernel & BSP

The buffer limit at shutdown needs Stefan's specification and storage stress testing, which is now automatic for any storage change. I'd target October as well, but if the stress results aren't clean in time, it can wait for November without much risk, because the other two changes already address most cases.

42 · Kyle JangField Quality

Should we consider an emergency update, given that this involves data loss? It's winter in the southern hemisphere now, and it will be winter in the north in a few months.

43 · Hannah OhField Quality

I thought about that. The rate is still low, about one in fifty thousand devices per month, and the service center repair procedure will protect most affected users' data starting next week. Southern winter ends in a few weeks, and the October update will be out well before northern winter. I think October is the right balance, as long as the service bulletin goes out on Monday.

Exposure on Other Devices

44 · Hannah OhField Quality

Is this only Orion? The boot policy and the low-battery logic are shared across our devices.

45 · Kyle JangField Quality

Orion Lite shows the same pattern, but about five times less often, because its storage uses the older firmware without the large buffer. Nova Tab is too new to have data. Pulse Watch has a different storage design and no reports.

46 · Yuna KwakKernel & BSP

The boot policy fix applies to every device anyway, and it's the most important one. I'll make it a common change, so Orion Lite and Nova Tab get it in October too. And for Atlas, it should be the default from day one.

Testing in the Cold

47 · Hannah OhField Quality

Let me ask the uncomfortable question. Why didn't our testing catch this before launch? We have power-cut rigs in the lab.

48 · Hyunsoo BaeKernel & BSP

We do, and they ran for weeks. But every one of those tests ran at room temperature, with a lab power supply instead of a real battery. At twenty-five degrees the battery estimate is accurate, so the phone always shuts down cleanly before the voltage collapses. The failure needs two things at once: a cold battery and a heavy write in progress.

49 · Stefan BrandtKestrel Memory

On our side, the firmware qualification also ran at room temperature for the flush-time measurement. The six hundred milliseconds only shows up when the buffer is full, and we didn't model a full buffer during a power cut. That's a gap in our test plan, and I'll own it.

50 · Yuna KwakKernel & BSP

I appreciate that. Here's what I'd propose. We move two of the power-cut rigs into the thermal chamber and run them at minus five degrees with real batteries. Each cycle fills the write buffer, drains the battery to the shutdown threshold, and checks whether the device boots cleanly afterwards.

51 · Hannah OhField Quality

How many cycles do we need before we trust the fix?

52 · Yuna KwakKernel & BSP

Before the fix, I'd expect to reproduce the corruption within a few hundred cycles. After the fix, I want at least five thousand clean cycles across both rigs. That's about four days of chamber time, so it fits before the October update freeze.

53 · Hyunsoo BaeKernel & BSP

And going forward, cold-temperature power-cut testing becomes a standard entry in the release checklist, not a special project. Atlas will run it from its first stable build.

Customer Communication

54 · Marcus LeeGlobal Service Operations

Before we wrap up, we need to decide what we tell customers. Most of these reports come from northern regions, and winter is only getting started there. I don't want service centers to be the first place people learn about this.

55 · Hannah OhField Quality

I agree, but we should be careful not to sound alarming. The rate is still low, and most users will never see it. A simple support article with practical advice is the right level for now.

56 · Marcus LeeGlobal Service Operations

Then the article says three things. Keep the phone charged above twenty percent in very cold weather, keep it in an inner pocket when you're outside, and if it doesn't start after a shutdown, bring it to a service center before trying a factory reset, because we can usually recover the data.

57 · Kyle JangField Quality

That last point matters most. In the reports we reviewed, a lot of the data loss happened because users reset the phone themselves. The repair script can't help after a reset.

58 · Marcus LeeGlobal Service Operations

I'll have the draft ready tomorrow and run it past legal. Once the software fix ships, we update the article to say the issue is resolved.

59 · Hannah OhField Quality

Let me summarize. Marcus: instruct service centers today to hold affected devices and offer loaners. 갑작스러운 종료가 데이터를 손상시키는 이유

13 · Hannah OhField Quality

그런데 폰은 늘 갑자기 전원이 꺼지잖아요. 왜 이 단말들에서는 파일 시스템이 손상되고 다른 단말들은 안 그렇죠?
