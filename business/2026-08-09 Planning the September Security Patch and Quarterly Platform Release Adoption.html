Planning the September Security Patch and Quarterly Platform Release Adoption

Agenda and Bulletin Overview

07 · Grace JungRelease Management

Thank you. We have two topics today. First, the September security bulletin, and specifically one critical vendor issue that could put our patch date at risk. 08 · Jiho NamProduct Security

Sure. The preview we received from Olivia's team lists thirty-eight vulnerabilities for September. In other words, it's a privilege escalation from a normal app to full control of the device. It's the most serious item in the whole bulletin, and right now, we don't have a fix for it.

The Critical GPU Vulnerability

11 · Grace JungRelease Management

Raj, what's the status on your side?

12 · Raj MehtaQorvix Product Security

Let me be upfront about the timeline. We confirmed the vulnerability three weeks ago. The obvious fix, a bounds check in the memory mapping path, turned out to break a performance optimization that several games rely on. So we had to redesign the fix. The new version is in internal testing now, and our current estimate for delivery to customers is September 3rd.

13 · Grace JungRelease Management

That's a problem for us. Our September maintenance build has a content cutoff of August 25th, so that SQE can complete validation and the carriers can approve it in time for the first week of September. A September 3rd delivery misses that by more than a week.

14 · Olivia GrantGoogle Partner Engineering

I should add the policy context here. The bulletin goes public on September 7th. After that, the vulnerability details are known, and the risk of exploitation goes up quickly, especially for a GPU issue like this one. We expect partners to ship critical fixes as soon as possible after publication. For devices in our security update program, the expectation is that the patch level reported by the device is accurate, meaning we can't claim the September level without including this fix.

15 · Jiho NamProduct Security

Right, and that's an important point. If we ship the September build without the GPU fix, we'd have to declare the August patch level, even though we include thirty-seven of the thirty-eight September fixes. That looks bad in every security report, and some enterprise customers have contractual requirements on the patch level.

16 · Raj MehtaQorvix Product Security

I understand the pressure. One option I can offer is an interim fix. We can deliver the original simple bounds check by August 20th. It closes the vulnerability completely, but it costs about eight percent in frame rate for the affected games. The full fix without the performance penalty would follow on September 3rd.

17 · Grace JungRelease Management

Eight percent is noticeable. Which games are we talking about?

18 · Raj MehtaQorvix Product Security

It mainly affects games that use a specific texture streaming technique. On our measurements, three of the top twenty games on your store are affected. Most users wouldn't notice in normal use, but enthusiasts who measure frame rates probably would.

19 · Sungmin YooProduct Security

From a security standpoint, I'd take the interim fix without hesitation. A temporary frame rate drop in three games is a much smaller problem than a known, public privilege escalation on millions of devices. We can replace it with the full fix in the October build.

20 · Grace JungRelease Management

I agree with the direction, but I want to make sure we're not trading one problem for another. If gamers notice a performance drop right after a security update, the story online becomes 'security update slows down games', and that can discourage people from updating at all. That's also a security risk, in a way.

21 · Kevin AlbrightGoogle Partner Engineering

That's a fair concern. What we've seen work for other partners is being transparent in the release notes without disclosing the vulnerability details: something like, this update includes security improvements to the graphics driver, and some games may see a minor performance change, which will be addressed in the next update. Users tend to accept that when it's stated up front.

Embargo and Disclosure Rules

22 · Olivia GrantGoogle Partner Engineering

Since we're talking about release notes, let me remind everyone of the embargo rules, because we had an incident with another partner last quarter. Until September 7th, you can ship the fix, but your release notes and any public communication must not describe the vulnerability. Shipping the patch early is fine. Describing it early is not.

23 · Jiho NamProduct Security

Understood. Our standard practice is that maintenance release notes before the bulletin date only mention the patch level and generic wording like 'security improvements'. After the bulletin is public, we publish our own security page with the full list of fixes.

24 · Grace JungRelease Management

25 · Olivia GrantGoogle Partner Engineering

Yes, and that's exactly where the incident happened last quarter. A partner published kernel source with a commit message that described the bug in detail, two weeks before the bulletin. Please make sure security fixes in your public source use neutral commit messages until the embargo lifts.

26 · Sungmin YooProduct Security

Good reminder. I'll check that our source release script strips or rewrites the messages for any commit tagged as security. If it doesn't, I'll get it fixed before the September build.

Quarterly Platform Release Adoption

27 · Grace JungRelease Management

Let's move to the second topic. Kevin, Google's quarterly platform release came out in June, and we haven't adopted it on Orion yet. What's your view?

28 · Kevin AlbrightGoogle Partner Engineering

Our recommendation is to adopt it. Besides new features, it includes a large number of stability fixes and some security hardening that doesn't appear in the monthly bulletins, because it's not tied to a specific vulnerability. Partners who skip quarterly releases tend to accumulate a gap that makes the next major upgrade harder.

29 · Grace JungRelease Management

I don't doubt the value. My concern is risk. A quarterly release is roughly a thousand changed files in the framework. Putting that into a maintenance release that also carries a critical security fix and an interim GPU workaround is a lot of change at once. If something breaks, it'll be hard to tell what caused it.

30 · Jiho NamProduct Security

I agree with Grace on not combining them in September. But from the security side, I don't want to skip the quarterly release entirely. Some of the hardening in it, especially around app permission checks, would close attack paths that we currently mitigate only partially.

31 · Kevin AlbrightGoogle Partner Engineering

Then what about adopting it in October? That would separate it from the September security build, and it would still put you on the latest quarterly base well before the next major release in December.

32 · Grace JungRelease Management

October works better for me, but there's a staffing issue. The framework team is already split between Atlas and the OS upgrade program, which kicked off yesterday. Merging a quarterly release in September means taking people away from one of those.

33 · Jiho NamProduct Security

Couldn't the OS upgrade program reuse that work? If the upgrade merge starts from a base that already includes the quarterly release, there would be fewer conflicts later. It's not entirely extra work.

34 · Kevin AlbrightGoogle Partner Engineering

That's generally true. Much of the quarterly release is already part of the next major version, so partners who take it first usually find the major merge somewhat easier.

35 · Sungmin YooProduct Security

Does the quarterly release require any vendor component updates? If Qorvix has to deliver new drivers for it, that's another dependency on a team that's already busy with the GPU fix.

36 · Kevin AlbrightGoogle Partner Engineering

It doesn't. Quarterly releases keep the vendor interface frozen, by design. Your existing vendor components work unchanged, which is one of the reasons they're relatively safe to adopt.

37 · Raj MehtaQorvix Product Security

I'm glad to hear that, honestly. My team has enough on its plate this month.

38 · Grace JungRelease Management

That's a good argument. I'll check with Brian and Elena whether the upgrade team can own the quarterly merge as their first step. If they agree, October is the plan. If not, I'll come back with an alternative rather than silently skipping it.

Carrier and Enterprise Considerations

39 · Jiho NamProduct Security

Before we finalize, there's one more stakeholder group we haven't mentioned: our enterprise customers. Several large companies that deploy Orion to their employees have security policies that block devices more than one patch level behind. If our September build reaches some carriers late, their devices could start getting blocked from corporate email in mid-October.

40 · Grace JungRelease Management

41 · Jiho NamProduct Security

Historically, two carriers in North America and one in Japan take three to four weeks. Everyone else is under two weeks. If we can get the September build to those three carriers by August 28th, we should be fine. If we submit in mid-September, their customers could fall behind.

42 · Olivia GrantGoogle Partner Engineering

For what it's worth, carriers are generally willing to prioritize a security-only maintenance release. If you tell them the build contains a critical fix and nothing else significant, many of them have a shorter review path. That's another reason not to bundle the quarterly release into September.

43 · Grace JungRelease Management

That's very useful to know. I'll ask our carrier team to request the expedited path for the September build with those three carriers, and to send them the submission on August 26th, the day after cutoff.

44 · Sungmin YooProduct Security

One more thing for the enterprise side. Some of those customers ask us directly, under a confidentiality agreement, whether the critical vulnerabilities affect our devices. Can we tell them before the bulletin is public, or does the embargo apply to them as well?

45 · Olivia GrantGoogle Partner Engineering

The embargo applies to them too, unless they're members of the advance notification program themselves. What you can say is that a security update is coming and that you recommend installing it as soon as it's available. That's usually enough for their planning.
