Investigating a System Hang during Long 4K Camera Recording

장시간 4K 카메라 녹화 중 시스템 hang 원인 분석

Orion에서 4K 60fps 동영상을 25~40분 이상 녹화하면 화면이 멈추고 워치독 리셋이 발생하는 이슈입니다. SQE, 사내 커널·BSP 팀, 칩셋 벤더 Qorvix 멀티미디어 팀, 이미지 센서 벤더 Lumicore가 램덤프 분석, 발열과의 연관성, 실험 계획과 수정 방향을 논의합니다.

Symptom and Reproduction

07 · Tom AhnSQE

Thanks. Soyeon, please walk us through what we've seen so far.

08 · Soyeon ParkSQE

Sure. The issue was first reported by a reviewer who was recording a long concert. On Orion, if you record 4K video at sixty frames per second continuously, after somewhere between twenty-five and forty minutes, the preview freezes. The device stops responding to touch and buttons, and about ten to fifteen seconds later, it reboots. The video file being recorded is usually corrupted, so the user loses the whole recording.

09 · Tom AhnSQE

Losing the recording is what makes this so painful. A crash at minute thirty-five of a concert is about as bad as it gets for a camera feature.

10 · Soyeon ParkSQE

Exactly. In the lab, we've reproduced it fourteen times out of thirty attempts on ten devices. A few details are important. First, it never happens at 4K thirty frames per second or at 1080p, even after two hours. Second, it happens faster when the room is warm: at thirty degrees Celsius ambient, it took an average of twenty-six minutes; at twenty-two degrees, it took thirty-eight minutes. Third, on the reset reason, twelve were watchdog barks with a ramdump, and two were watchdog bites with no dump.

11 · Kenji WatanabeLumicore Sensor Engineering

Do you have the sensor temperature at the time of the freeze? Our sensor reports its internal temperature through a register, and your camera software logs it, I believe.

12 · Soyeon ParkSQE

We do. In every case, the sensor temperature was between seventy-four and seventy-nine degrees at the moment of the freeze. In the non-failing runs, it peaked around seventy-one degrees.

Ramdump Analysis

13 · Tom AhnSQE

Yuna, Junho, what do the dumps show?

14 · Yuna KwakKernel & BSP

I followed the same order I described on Friday. The panic reason is the hardware watchdog bark. When I look at the CPUs, seven of the eight are waiting to acquire the same spinlock, and they're all spinning with interrupts disabled. The eighth CPU, CPU three, holds that spinlock, and it's inside the ISP driver's error recovery function, polling a hardware status register in a loop.

15 · Junho KimKernel & BSP

And that explains why the whole system freezes, not just the camera. Once CPU three holds the lock with interrupts off and never lets go, every other CPU that touches the camera path piles up behind it. Eventually, nothing can pet the watchdog. In the two bite cases, my guess is that the CPU handling the watchdog interrupt was also one of the CPUs spinning with interrupts disabled, so the bark couldn't be handled.

16 · Tom AhnSQE

What's the error recovery function waiting for?

17 · Junho KimKernel & BSP

It's waiting for the ISP to report that its input pipeline has drained after an error. Just before the freeze, the kernel log shows a burst of errors from the camera serial interface receiver: checksum errors and frame synchronization errors on the link between the sensor and the ISP. The ISP raises an error interrupt, the driver enters recovery, takes the lock, and waits for the pipeline to drain. But the pipeline never drains, and there's no timeout in that loop.

18 · Yuna KwakKernel & BSP

So we have two layers again, like the storage panic last week. There's a trigger, the link errors at high temperature, and there's a software defect, an unbounded wait with interrupts disabled, that turns a recoverable error into a full system hang.

Discussing the Trigger

19 · Tom AhnSQE

Kenji, does the sensor behave differently at those temperatures?

20 · Kenji WatanabeLumicore Sensor Engineering

The sensor is specified to operate up to eighty-five degrees, so seventy-nine is within specification. However, I should be transparent that signal quality on the high-speed output link does degrade as temperature rises. At 4K sixty, the link runs at its highest data rate, which leaves the smallest margin. So I'm not surprised that errors appear only in that mode. But in our reference design, the error rate at seventy-nine degrees is extremely low, perhaps one corrupted packet per hour.

21 · Ming HuangQorvix Multimedia

One corrupted packet per hour would be fine. The ISP is designed to drop a bad frame and continue. What we're seeing in these logs is different: dozens of errors within a few milliseconds. That looks more like the link losing synchronization completely, which the ISP treats as a fatal input error.

22 · Kenji WatanabeLumicore Sensor Engineering

Complete loss of synchronization would surprise me with the sensor alone. I wonder whether the board layout plays a role. The trace length and the connector on Orion are different from our reference design. Has your hardware team measured the eye diagram on that link at high temperature?

23 · Tom AhnSQE

I don't believe so, at least not at seventy-nine degrees. I'll ask them. But I want to be careful not to turn this into a hardware debate that takes a month. Even if the link is marginal, we can't change the board on devices that are already in customers' hands.

24 · Junho KimKernel & BSP

Right. Whatever the trigger is, the software has to survive it. The worst acceptable outcome for a link failure should be a few dropped frames, or at most a stopped recording with the file saved properly. A full system hang is never acceptable.

Software Fix Options

25 · Tom AhnSQE

Ming, why doesn't the pipeline drain? Is there a way to force it?

26 · Ming HuangQorvix Multimedia

When the input link loses synchronization in the middle of a frame, the ISP's input stage can get stuck waiting for the end of that frame, which never arrives. In that state, the drain never completes. The documented way out is a hardware reset of the ISP input block, followed by reprogramming the pipeline. Our reference driver does that after a timeout of one hundred milliseconds. It looks like the Orion driver lost that timeout at some point.

27 · Junho KimKernel & BSP

That's a little embarrassing, but I think you're right. I checked the history this morning. Last year, we restructured the recovery function to support a multi-camera mode, and the timeout was dropped during the rework. It wasn't caught in review because the old code was spread across two functions. I'll own that.

28 · Yuna KwakKernel & BSP

I'd go one step further than restoring the timeout. Polling a hardware register in a loop while holding a spinlock with interrupts disabled is risky even with a timeout, because one hundred milliseconds is a very long time for seven other CPUs to spin. I'd restructure it: mark the pipeline as being in recovery, release the lock, do the wait outside the lock with interrupts enabled, then reset and take the lock again only for the short reprogramming step.

29 · Ming HuangQorvix Multimedia

That's a better design, and it's closer to what our newer driver generation does. The only complication is that other parts of the driver must check the 'in recovery' state and avoid touching the hardware during that window. I can share the pattern from our newer driver to save Junho some time.

30 · Tom AhnSQE

How do the two options compare in risk and time? I'm thinking about the maintenance release schedule.

31 · Junho KimKernel & BSP

Restoring the timeout is a small change, maybe twenty lines, and I could have it ready tomorrow. Yuna's restructuring is better long-term, but it's a few hundred lines and touches locking across the driver. I'd estimate a week to write and another week of testing. My suggestion is to do both in sequence: the timeout fix goes into the September maintenance release as a quick mitigation, and the restructuring goes into October after proper testing.

32 · Yuna KwakKernel & BSP

I can support that, as long as the October change doesn't quietly slip to November. Temporary fixes have a way of becoming permanent.

Protecting the User's Recording

33 · Soyeon ParkSQE

There's another angle I'd like us to cover. Even with the timeout fix, the ISP reset will interrupt the video stream. What happens to the recording? If the camera app crashes and the file is still corrupted, users will still lose their concert.

34 · Junho KimKernel & BSP

Good point. After the reset, the driver reports an error to the camera service. As far as I know, the current app behavior is to stop the recording and show an error. Whether the file is finalized properly depends on the framework's recorder, which isn't my area.

35 · Tom AhnSQE

Then we need the framework camera team in the loop. Soyeon, can you test what happens to the file when we inject a link error during recording on a build with the timeout fix? If the file is unplayable, that's a separate issue for the framework team, and it needs to be fixed in the same release.

36 · Ming HuangQorvix Multimedia

For error injection, our ISP has a debug register that simulates loss of synchronization on the input link. I'll send Junho the instructions. It's much faster than heating the device in a thermal chamber for half an hour every time.

37 · Kenji WatanabeLumicore Sensor Engineering

Ideally, the camera could also reduce the risk before the error happens. When the sensor temperature crosses seventy-five degrees, the camera app could warn the user or step down to 4K thirty frames per second. That's what several other device makers do with our sensor.

38 · Tom AhnSQE

That's a product decision, so I'll raise it with the camera product owner rather than decide it here. But I'll include it in the summary as a recommendation from Lumicore.

Exposure on Other Models

39 · Tom AhnSQE

Before we talk about validation, are any other products exposed? The ISP driver is shared across several of our devices.

40 · Junho KimKernel & BSP

The restructured recovery function from last year is used on Orion and on the upcoming Orion Fold. Orion Lite uses an older ISP and a different driver, so it isn't affected. Orion Fold is the bigger concern, because it supports 4K sixty on two of its cameras, and it has less room for heat to escape in the folded position.

41 · Ming HuangQorvix Multimedia

Orion Fold also uses a newer revision of our ISP, but the input stage behaves the same way when synchronization is lost, so I would assume it's vulnerable until proven otherwise.

42 · Tom AhnSQE

Then the timeout fix has to go into the Orion Fold launch software as well. I'll flag it to Sarah's launch team today, since they're tracking every change that goes into their final builds.

43 · Soyeon ParkSQE

I'd also like to run the original thermal scenario on a few Orion Fold prototypes, folded and unfolded. If the fold position makes the sensor hotter, we'll see the issue sooner there, and that would be useful data for the hardware team too.

Validation Plan

44 · Tom AhnSQE

Let's agree on how we'll validate the fix. Soyeon?

45 · Soyeon ParkSQE

I propose three steps. First, error injection on ten devices, a hundred injections each, with the timeout fix. Pass criteria: zero system hangs, and the device remains responsive. Second, the original scenario: one-hour 4K sixty recordings at thirty degrees ambient, twenty runs. Pass criteria: zero hangs, and if the recording stops, the file must be playable. Third, our standard camera regression suite to make sure we didn't break anything else.

46 · Yuna KwakKernel & BSP

For the error injection step, please also enable the lock debugging options on two of the devices. If there's any other path that waits too long with the lock held, lock debugging will tell us directly, instead of us finding it the hard way in the field.

47 · Soyeon ParkSQE

Will do. With the build tomorrow, I can finish the first two steps by Thursday and the regression suite by Monday.

Ming: send the error injection instructions and the recovery pattern from your newer driver today. Yuna: design the restructured recovery with Junho, target the October release. Soyeon: run the three-step validation, results by Monday. Kenji: share your reference link measurements at high temperature, so our hardware team has a baseline. 증상과 재현

감사합니다. 소연 님, 지금까지 본 것을 설명해 주세요.

네. 이 이슈는 긴 콘서트를 녹화하던 한 리뷰어가 처음 보고했습니다. Orion에서 4K 60fps 동영상을 연속으로 녹화하면 25분에서 40분 사이에 프리뷰가 멈춥니다. 단말이 터치와 버튼에 반응하지 않고, 10~15초 후 재부팅됩니다. 녹화 중이던 동영상 파일은 보통 손상되기 때문에 사용자는 녹화 전체를 잃습니다.

램덤프 분석

13 · Tom AhnSQE

유나 님, 준호 님, 덤프에는 뭐가 나오나요?

14 · Yuna KwakKernel & BSP

금요일에 설명한 순서 그대로 따랐습니다. 패닉 원인은 하드웨어 워치독 bark입니다. CPU를 보면 8개 중 7개가 같은 스핀락을 얻으려고 기다리고 있고, 모두 인터럽트가 비활성화된 상태로 스핀하고 있습니다. 여덟 번째 CPU인 3번 CPU가 그 스핀락을 잡고 있는데, ISP 드라이버의 에러 복구 함수 안에서 하드웨어 상태 레지스터를 루프로 폴링하고 있습니다.

15 · Junho KimKernel & BSP

그래서 카메라만이 아니라 시스템 전체가 멈추는 거죠. 3번 CPU가 인터럽트를 끈 채 락을 잡고 절대 놓지 않으면, 카메라 경로를 건드리는 다른 모든 CPU가 그 뒤에 쌓입니다. 결국 아무도 워치독을 쓰다듬지 못하게 됩니다. bite 두 건의 경우, 워치독 인터럽트를 처리해야 할 CPU도 인터럽트를 끈 채 스핀하던 CPU 중 하나여서 bark를 처리하지 못한 것 같습니다.

16 · Tom AhnSQE

에러 복구 함수는 뭘 기다리고 있나요?

17 · Junho KimKernel & BSP

에러 후 ISP가 입력 파이프라인이 비워졌다고 보고하기를 기다리고 있습니다. 멈추기 직전에 커널 로그를 보면 카메라 시리얼 인터페이스 수신부에서 에러가 연달아 발생합니다. 센서와 ISP 사이 링크에서의 체크섬 에러와 프레임 동기화 에러입니다. ISP가 에러 인터럽트를 올리면 드라이버가 복구에 들어가서 락을 잡고 파이프라인이 비워지기를 기다립니다. 그런데 파이프라인은 끝내 비워지지 않고, 그 루프에는 타임아웃이 없습니다.

18 · Yuna KwakKernel & BSP

그러니까 지난주 스토리지 패닉처럼 또 두 계층이 있네요. 트리거는 고온에서의 링크 에러이고, 소프트웨어 결함은 인터럽트를 끈 채 끝없이 기다리는 것으로, 복구 가능한 에러를 시스템 전체 hang으로 바꿔 버립니다.

트리거에 대한 논의

19 · Tom AhnSQE

Kenji, 그 온도에서 센서가 다르게 동작하나요?

20 · Kenji WatanabeLumicore Sensor Engineering

센서는 85도까지 동작하도록 규격이 정해져 있어서 79도는 규격 범위 안입니다. 다만 솔직히 말씀드리면, 온도가 올라가면 고속 출력 링크의 신호 품질이 저하되는 건 사실입니다. 4K 60fps에서는 링크가 최고 데이터 속도로 동작해서 여유가 가장 작습니다. 그래서 그 모드에서만 에러가 나타나는 건 놀랍지 않습니다. 하지만 저희 레퍼런스 설계에서는 79도에서의 에러율이 극히 낮아서, 시간당 손상 패킷이 한 개 정도입니다.

21 · Ming HuangQorvix Multimedia

시간당 손상 패킷 한 개는 괜찮습니다. ISP는 불량 프레임을 버리고 계속 진행하도록 설계돼 있습니다. 이 로그에서 보이는 건 다릅니다. 수 밀리초 안에 수십 개의 에러가 나옵니다. 그건 링크가 동기화를 완전히 잃는 쪽에 가깝고, ISP는 그걸 치명적인 입력 에러로 처리합니다.

22 · Kenji WatanabeLumicore Sensor Engineering
