Agreeing on a Monthly Kernel Update Cadence with Google and Qorvix

Google, Qorvix와 월간 커널 업데이트 주기 합의

USB use-after-free 사례를 계기로, Orion과 Orion Lite의 커널 업데이트를 분기 단위에서 월 단위로 바꾸는 방안을 논의하는 회의입니다. 사내 커널·BSP, SQE, Google 안드로이드 커널팀, 칩셋 벤더 Qorvix가 GKI 월간 릴리스 적용 방식, 벤더 모듈 호환성, 커널 중심 회귀 테스트, 시범 적용 일정을 합의합니다.

01 / English script

Opening

01 · Jiwoo LeeKernel & BSP

Good morning, everyone, and good evening to Nathan in Seattle. Thank you for joining on a Sunday. Last week, we found a USB crash on Orion that had already been fixed upstream three weeks earlier. We simply hadn't picked it up, because we only merge kernel updates once a quarter. Today, I'd like us to agree on a faster cadence.

02 · Chris MoonKernel & BSP

Chris, Kernel and BSP. I cover Orion Lite and Atlas Lite, so whatever we decide here affects my products too.

03 · Tom AhnSQE

Tom, SQE. I'm here to make sure that 'faster' doesn't mean 'less tested'.

04 · Nathan BrooksGoogle Android Kernel Team

Nathan Brooks, Android kernel team at Google. I'm happy to see this conversation happening. A monthly cadence is what we recommend to all partners.

05 · Laura ChenQorvix Customer Engineering

Laura Chen, Qorvix customer engineering. Most of my time is on storage and display these days, but I'm covering the kernel relationship for Orion this month.

How We Update Today

06 · Jiwoo LeeKernel & BSP

Let me describe our current process, so we all start from the same picture. Orion and Orion Lite run the generic kernel image for the 6.1 branch. Google publishes a new certified build of that image every month, containing all the upstream stable fixes and security patches. Today, we take one of those builds once a quarter, rebuild our vendor modules against it, and run our full kernel regression, which takes about three weeks.

07 · Nathan BrooksGoogle Android Kernel Team

One small correction, if I may. You shouldn't need to rebuild your vendor modules for each monthly image. The kernel module interface is frozen for the life of the branch. Modules built against the first 6.1 image should load and work on every later 6.1 image without being rebuilt. That's the whole point of the stable interface.

08 · Jiwoo LeeKernel & BSP

In theory, yes. In practice, we rebuild because two of our modules still use symbols that were added to the list after launch. They load fine, but our build system treats each image update as a full rebuild. That's a habit from the pre-GKI days, more than a real requirement.

09 · Tom AhnSQE

And the three-week regression is the real bottleneck. It's the same suite we run for a full maintenance release: functional tests, performance, battery, and a long stress campaign. Running that every month for every model would take most of the kernel test capacity we have.

Google's Testing and Our Testing

10 · Nathan BrooksGoogle Android Kernel Team

Let me explain what happens on our side, because it may change how much you need to repeat. Each monthly image goes through our own regression on a large device fleet, including reference hardware from several chipset vendors. It also runs on early partner devices for two weeks before it's published. So the core kernel changes are already well tested by the time you receive them.

11 · Tom AhnSQE

I believe that, but your fleet doesn't include our devices, our modules, or our workloads. The storage panic this month only happened with our specific firmware and our specific timeout. No amount of testing on reference hardware would have caught it.

12 · Nathan BrooksGoogle Android Kernel Team

That's a fair point, and I agree. The question isn't whether you test, but what you test. Our testing covers the core kernel well. Your testing should focus on the interaction between the core kernel and your modules, under your workloads. That's a much smaller and more targeted suite than a full release regression.

13 · Tom AhnSQE

That's actually close to what we proposed at the GKI tech share two weeks ago: a kernel-focused suite that runs in about twelve hours, with stress profiles for storage, USB, display, and power, on fifty devices per model. We've built a first version of it.

14 · Chris MoonKernel & BSP

Has it run yet? I'd like to know whether it would have caught the two kernel issues we had this month.

15 · Tom AhnSQE

We ran it retroactively last week, on the builds from before each fix. It caught the USB crash in nine hours, through the plug and unplug rig. It caught the storage panic in eleven hours. So, yes, both would have been caught before release.

16 · Jiwoo LeeKernel & BSP

That's very convincing. Twelve hours a month per model is something we can actually afford.

The Vendor Side

17 · Laura ChenQorvix Customer Engineering

From Qorvix's side, I want to raise one concern. Our modules are tested against specific kernel images. If you update the core kernel every month, and something breaks in one of our drivers, we need to know which image introduced the problem. Otherwise, we'll spend days arguing about whether it's our bug or a kernel change.

18 · Nathan BrooksGoogle Android Kernel Team

That's exactly why monthly is better than quarterly for that problem, not worse. With a monthly update, if something breaks, the suspect is one month of changes, maybe a few hundred commits. With a quarterly update, it's three months, over a thousand commits, and bisecting becomes much harder.

19 · Laura ChenQorvix Customer Engineering

That's a good argument. In that case, I'd ask for one thing: send us the kernel-focused suite results for each monthly update, even when everything passes. If our test team can see the trend, we can spot problems in our drivers before they become failures.

20 · Tom AhnSQE

That's easy. We generate a report anyway. I'll add Qorvix to the distribution list.

21 · Chris MoonKernel & BSP

One more vendor question. Qorvix also delivers its own driver fixes, separate from the core kernel. Today, those come whenever they're ready. Could we align them to the same monthly rhythm, so we test everything together once a month instead of in random batches?

22 · Laura ChenQorvix Customer Engineering

For routine fixes, yes. We can deliver a monthly driver bundle about a week before your kernel update window. Critical security fixes would still come immediately, of course, as they do now.

Risk and Rollback

23 · Tom AhnSQE

What happens if a monthly image causes a problem that our suite doesn't catch, and it reaches users? With quarterly updates, at least we'd been running the image for weeks before shipping it.

24 · Jiwoo LeeKernel & BSP

Two safety nets. First, the monthly image doesn't ship on its own; it rides along with our normal monthly maintenance release, which still goes through the full staged rollout and halt criteria. Second, because the core kernel is a separate image, we can roll back just the kernel to the previous month's version in the next update, without touching anything else.

25 · Nathan BrooksGoogle Android Kernel Team

And from our side, if a regression is found in a monthly image, we publish a fixed image quickly, usually within a week. Partners who report problems early get them fixed for everyone. Your field data would actually help us improve the image for all partners.

26 · Chris MoonKernel & BSP

That's a nice side benefit. Last week, Nathan's crash signature list saved us a week. If we're on recent images, the reverse can happen too: our reports can save other partners time.

27 · Tom AhnSQE

I'd like one more safety net on our side. For the first three monthly updates, let's start the staged rollout at half a percent and hold it for seventy-two hours, just like we agreed for security-related firmware. Once we've seen a few clean months, we can go back to the normal rollout speed.

28 · Jiwoo LeeKernel & BSP

Agreed. It costs us a few days per release, and it gives everyone more confidence while the process is new. I'll add it to the pilot plan.

29 · Laura ChenQorvix Customer Engineering

From Qorvix's side, if a rollout is paused because of something in one of our drivers, please call me directly. We'd rather start debugging on the same day than wait for a ticket to be triaged.

The Pilot Plan

30 · Jiwoo LeeKernel & BSP

Let me propose a concrete plan. In September, we run a dry run: we take the latest monthly image, run the kernel-focused suite on Orion and Orion Lite, and see how long it really takes end to end, without shipping it. In October, the first real monthly image goes into the Orion October maintenance release. If October goes well, Orion Lite follows in November. After three months, we review the results.

31 · Tom AhnSQE

I support that. The dry run will tell us whether twelve hours is realistic, including flashing, setup, and analyzing the results. My guess is that the first one will take closer to two days, and we'll get faster with practice.

32 · Chris MoonKernel & BSP

What would make us stop the pilot? I'd like us to define that up front, rather than arguing about it later.

33 · Jiwoo LeeKernel & BSP

Good point. I'd propose: if a monthly image causes a field regression that our suite should have caught, we pause and improve the suite before continuing. If two monthly images in a row cause field regressions, we return to quarterly updates and rethink the approach.

34 · Nathan BrooksGoogle Android Kernel Team

That sounds very reasonable. I'd be surprised if you hit either condition, but it's good to have them written down.

Fitting into the Release Calendar

35 · Chris MoonKernel & BSP

Let's talk about timing within the month. Our maintenance releases have a content cutoff around the twenty-fifth. When does Google publish each monthly image, and does that leave enough time for our suite?

36 · Nathan BrooksGoogle Android Kernel Team

Monthly images are usually published in the second week of the month. So if you pick up the image around the tenth, you have about two weeks before your cutoff. That should be plenty for a twelve-hour suite, even with a rerun or two.

37 · Tom AhnSQE

Two weeks works for us. My only concern is collisions with launches. In September, the stress lab is mostly booked for Orion Fold. If the dry run and a launch need the lab in the same week, the launch will win, and the kernel suite will slip.

38 · Jiwoo LeeKernel & BSP

Then let's reserve a permanent slot: fifty devices per model, every month, from the tenth to the fifteenth, protected from launch requests. If kernel updates are going to be routine, the lab capacity has to be routine too.

39 · Tom AhnSQE

I can do that, but I'll need Grace's support at the release council, because launch teams will push back. I'll present it together with the retroactive results, since they show clearly what the suite would have prevented this month.

40 · Laura ChenQorvix Customer Engineering

If the slot is fixed, it also helps us. We can plan our monthly driver bundle around it and know exactly when our code will be tested. Predictability is worth a lot to a vendor with many customers.

Atlas and the New Products

41 · Chris MoonKernel & BSP

For Atlas and Atlas Lite, which launch on 6.12, should we follow the same monthly model from launch, or wait until they're stable?

42 · Nathan BrooksGoogle Android Kernel Team

I'd strongly recommend starting from launch. The first months after launch are when the most fixes land upstream for a new branch, because that's when the whole ecosystem is finding bugs at the same time. Partners who wait often end up six months behind on exactly the fixes that matter most.

43 · Jiwoo LeeKernel & BSP

That makes sense. And because Atlas will be built with the module build changes from day one, the monthly update should be cheaper there than on Orion.

44 · Tom AhnSQE

I'll make sure the kernel-focused suite is part of the Atlas test plan from the first proto build, so by launch, we'll have six months of baseline data to compare each update against.

45 · Laura ChenQorvix Customer Engineering

Qorvix will do the same for Atlas Lite. I'll make sure our Q7 team plans their monthly bundle from the first drop, not from launch.

Measuring the Benefit

46 · Chris MoonKernel & BSP

How will we show management that this was worth the effort? Monthly updates mean more work for kernel and SQE every month.

47 · Jiwoo LeeKernel & BSP

I'd track two numbers. First, the age of the kernel fixes we ship: today, the average fix reaches our users about four months after it's published upstream. With monthly updates, it should be under six weeks. Second, the number of field kernel crashes that match a signature already fixed upstream. Last quarter, that was three of our top ten kernel crashes.

48 · Tom AhnSQE

Three of the top ten crashes being already fixed upstream is a strong argument by itself. Those are crashes our users suffered for no good reason.

49 · Nathan BrooksGoogle Android Kernel Team

If it helps, I can share anonymized data on how field crash rates changed for partners who moved from quarterly to monthly. It's been a clear improvement for most of them.

Decisions and Action Items

50 · Jiwoo LeeKernel & BSP

Let me summarize. We'll move Orion and Orion Lite to monthly kernel image updates. September is a dry run. October is the first real update for Orion, and November for Orion Lite. Stop conditions as agreed. Actions: Tom finalizes the kernel-focused suite and runs the dry run. I'll clean up the two modules that use late symbols and change the build so we stop rebuilding modules for every image. Laura arranges the monthly Qorvix driver bundle. Nathan shares the partner data. Chris prepares Orion Lite for November.

51 · Laura ChenQorvix Customer Engineering

I'll confirm the monthly bundle schedule with our release team this week, and send it to Jiwoo and Tom in writing.

52 · Chris MoonKernel & BSP

And I'll make sure Atlas Lite starts on this model from day one, so we never build the quarterly habit there.

Closing

53 · Jiwoo LeeKernel & BSP

Thank you, everyone. I think this is one of the most valuable process changes we've made this year. We'll review the dry run results at the next vendor sync, on Sunday, September 13th, at 10 a.m. Korea time.

54 · Nathan BrooksGoogle Android Kernel Team

Thanks for having me. I'm looking forward to seeing the dry run numbers. If anything in the dry run looks strange, send it to me before the thirteenth, and I'll check whether other partners have seen the same thing.

55 · Tom AhnSQE

I'll book the stress lab for the dry run before anyone else claims it.

56 · Chris MoonKernel & BSP

And I'll write a one-page summary of today's decisions for the release council, including the stop conditions and the protected lab slot. If the council understands the reasoning before Tom asks for the slot, the conversation will be much easier.

57 · Laura ChenQorvix Customer Engineering

Thank you all. This is the kind of process change that makes a vendor's life easier, not harder, which I don't get to say very often.

현재 업데이트 방식

06 · Jiwoo LeeKernel & BSP

모두 같은 그림에서 시작하도록 현재 절차를 설명하겠습니다. Orion과 Orion Lite는 6.1 브랜치의 GKI로 동작합니다. Google은 매달 업스트림 안정 수정과 보안 패치를 모두 포함한 그 이미지의 새 인증 빌드를 공개합니다. 지금 저희는 분기에 한 번 그중 하나를 가져와 벤더 모듈을 다시 빌드하고, 약 3주 걸리는 전체 커널 회귀 테스트를 돌립니다.

07 · Nathan BrooksGoogle Android Kernel Team

괜찮다면 작은 정정 하나 하겠습니다. 월간 이미지마다 벤더 모듈을 다시 빌드할 필요는 없어야 합니다. 커널 모듈 인터페이스는 브랜치 수명 동안 고정돼 있습니다. 첫 6.1 이미지 기준으로 빌드된 모듈은 다시 빌드하지 않아도 이후 모든 6.1 이미지에서 로드되고 동작해야 합니다. 그게 안정 인터페이스의 핵심이죠.

08 · Jiwoo LeeKernel & BSP

이론상으로는 그렇습니다. 실제로는 모듈 두 개가 출시 이후 리스트에 추가된 심볼을 아직 쓰고 있어서 다시 빌드합니다. 로드는 잘 되지만, 저희 빌드 시스템이 이미지 업데이트를 매번 전체 재빌드로 처리합니다. 실제 요구사항이라기보다 GKI 이전 시절의 습관이죠.

09 · Tom AhnSQE

그리고 3주짜리 회귀 테스트가 진짜 병목입니다. 전체 유지보수 릴리스에 돌리는 것과 같은 스위트로, 기능 테스트, 성능, 배터리, 긴 스트레스 테스트가 들어갑니다. 모든 모델에 대해 매달 그걸 돌리면 저희가 가진 커널 테스트 역량 대부분을 쓰게 됩니다.

Google의 테스트와 우리의 테스트

10 · Nathan BrooksGoogle Android Kernel Team

저희 쪽에서 무슨 일이 일어나는지 설명하겠습니다. 그러면 귀사가 반복해야 할 범위가 달라질 수 있습니다. 월간 이미지마다 여러 칩셋 벤더의 레퍼런스 하드웨어를 포함한 대규모 단말군에서 저희 자체 회귀 테스트를 거칩니다. 공개 전에 초기 파트너 단말에서도 2주간 돌아가고요. 그래서 핵심 커널 변경은 귀사가 받을 때쯤 이미 잘 테스트돼 있습니다.

11 · Tom AhnSQE

그건 믿지만, 그쪽 단말군에는 저희 단말, 저희 모듈, 저희 작업 부하가 없습니다. 이번 달 스토리지 패닉은 저희 특정 펌웨어와 저희 특정 타임아웃에서만 발생했습니다. 레퍼런스 하드웨어에서 아무리 테스트해도 그건 잡지 못했을 겁니다.

12 · Nathan BrooksGoogle Android Kernel Team

타당한 지적이고 동의합니다. 문제는 테스트를 하느냐가 아니라 무엇을 테스트하느냐입니다. 저희 테스트는 핵심 커널을 잘 다룹니다. 귀사 테스트는 귀사 작업 부하에서 핵심 커널과 귀사 모듈 사이의 상호작용에 집중해야 합니다. 그건 전체 릴리스 회귀 테스트보다 훨씬 작고 목표가 분명한 스위트입니다.

13 · Tom AhnSQE

그건 사실 2주 전 GKI 기술 공유에서 저희가 제안한 것과 비슷합니다. 모델당 단말 50대로 스토리지, USB, 디스플레이, 전원 스트레스 프로필을 포함해 약 12시간 걸리는 커널 중심 스위트요. 첫 버전을 만들어 뒀습니다.

14 · Chris MoonKernel & BSP

벌써 돌려 봤나요? 이번 달에 있었던 커널 이슈 두 개를 그게 잡았을지 알고 싶습니다.

15 · Tom AhnSQE

지난주에 각 수정 이전 빌드로 거슬러 올라가 돌려 봤습니다. USB 크래시는 연결·해제 장치로 9시간 만에 잡았습니다. 스토리지 패닉은 11시간 만에 잡았고요. 그러니 네, 둘 다 출시 전에 잡혔을 겁니다.

16 · Jiwoo LeeKernel & BSP

아주 설득력 있네요. 모델당 한 달에 12시간이면 저희가 실제로 감당할 수 있는 수준입니다.

벤더 측면

17 · Laura ChenQorvix Customer Engineering

Qorvix 쪽에서 우려 하나를 제기하고 싶습니다. 저희 모듈은 특정 커널 이미지를 기준으로 테스트됩니다. 핵심 커널을 매달 업데이트하다가 저희 드라이버 하나에서 뭔가 깨지면, 어떤 이미지가 문제를 일으켰는지 알아야 합니다. 그렇지 않으면 저희 버그인지 커널 변경인지를 두고 며칠씩 논쟁하게 될 겁니다.

18 · Nathan BrooksGoogle Android Kernel Team

바로 그래서 그 문제에 대해서는 월간이 분기보다 나쁜 게 아니라 낫습니다. 월간 업데이트라면 뭔가 깨졌을 때 용의자는 한 달 치 변경, 많아야 수백 개 커밋입니다. 분기 업데이트라면 3개월 치, 천 개가 넘는 커밋이고, bisect가 훨씬 어려워집니다.

19 · Laura ChenQorvix Customer Engineering

좋은 논거네요. 그렇다면 하나만 요청하겠습니다. 모든 게 통과하더라도 월간 업데이트마다 커널 중심 스위트 결과를 보내 주세요. 저희 테스트팀이 추세를 볼 수 있으면, 실패가 되기 전에 저희 드라이버의 문제를 발견할 수 있습니다.

쉬운 일입니다. 어차피 보고서를 생성하니까요. Qorvix를 배포 목록에 추가하겠습니다.

21 · Chris MoonKernel & BSP
