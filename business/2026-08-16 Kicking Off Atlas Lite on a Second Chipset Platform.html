Kicking Off Atlas Lite on a Second Chipset Platform

두 번째 칩셋 기반 Atlas Lite 개발 킥오프

Atlas의 소프트웨어를 Qorvix의 중급 칩셋으로 옮겨 내년 6월에 출시할 Atlas Lite의 킥오프 회의입니다. 프로그램 관리, 커널·BSP, 플랫폼 프레임워크, SQE 팀이 개발 시작 시점, 공통 브랜치 전략, 메모리 제약, Atlas와의 인력·테스트 자원 충돌을 고려한 마일스톤을 합의합니다.

01 / English script

Attendance and Introductions

01 · Mark YoonProgram Management

Good afternoon, everyone. Thanks for coming in on a Sunday. I'll be quick with the introductions, because I want most of our time for the schedule discussion. I'm Mark from Program Management, and I've just been assigned as the software program manager for Atlas Lite. It's my first program on the system software side, so please be patient with me when I ask basic questions.

02 · Chris MoonKernel & BSP

Welcome, Mark. Basic questions are usually the ones that catch real problems. I'm Chris, Kernel and BSP. I'll be the kernel lead for Atlas Lite, on top of my Orion Lite work.

03 · Dongwook KangKernel & BSP

Dongwook Kang, also Kernel and BSP. I just finished the Pulse Watch launch, so I'm free to focus on Atlas Lite bring-up from next week.

04 · Harin JeonPlatform Framework

Harin Jeon, Platform Framework. I'll lead the framework work for Atlas Lite. Most of my team is currently on Atlas, so I'm especially interested in how we share code between the two programs.

05 · Nina ChoSQE

Nina Cho, SQE. I'll be the quality lead. I was on the Nova Tab launch until last week, so I've had a very recent reminder of how things go wrong at the end of a program.

Program Background

06 · Mark YoonProgram Management

Let me give the background. Atlas Lite is the mid-range companion to Atlas. The business wants it to share the Atlas software experience, same Android version and same major features, but on Qorvix's new mid-range chipset, the Q7, to hit a lower price point. The target market launch is June 4th, 2027, about three months after Atlas.

07 · Chris MoonKernel & BSP

And that's the core challenge right there. Atlas is on Arvenna. Atlas Lite is on Qorvix. So the kernel and BSP work is almost entirely separate, even though the user sees the same software. Two chipset vendors means two BSPs, two sets of drivers, and two sets of vendor bugs.

08 · Mark YoonProgram Management

That's exactly the kind of thing I need to understand. Are there any advantages to using Qorvix again, given that Orion and Orion Lite are also on Qorvix?

09 · Dongwook KangKernel & BSP

Definitely. We know the Qorvix team, their release process, and their tools. Many of our Orion drivers for displays, sensors, and charging can be reused with modest changes. And the Q7 is a cost-reduced version of the chipset family Orion uses, so a lot of the hardware blocks are familiar. The risk is lower than starting with a brand-new vendor.

10 · Chris MoonKernel & BSP

One important difference, though. Orion is on kernel 6.1, but Atlas Lite has to launch on 6.12, because it's a new device on the new Android release. So we can reuse Orion's driver logic, but not Orion's kernel. That's where the GKI work Sora presented on Friday becomes directly relevant.

Software Platform Strategy

11 · Mark YoonProgram Management

Let's talk about how the code is organized. Harin, do we create a separate branch for Atlas Lite, or do both products live on one branch?

12 · Harin JeonPlatform Framework

My strong preference is one common branch for the framework, with device-specific configuration layered on top. We tried separate branches for Orion and Orion Lite, and we spent months porting fixes back and forth. Half of the field issues on Orion Lite last year were bugs we had already fixed on Orion but never ported.

13 · Nina ChoSQE

From the quality side, I fully agree. One branch means one set of fixes. But it also means that a change made for Atlas can break Atlas Lite, and vice versa. If we go that way, we need automated testing on both devices for every change, from the very beginning.

14 · Chris MoonKernel & BSP

For the kernel, the situation is a little different. With GKI, the core kernel is shared anyway. Our vendor modules will be different for each chipset, but common logic, like our thermal framework or charging policies, should live in shared modules that both devices use. I'd want a clear rule: if code isn't chipset-specific, it goes into a shared module.

15 · Mark YoonProgram Management

That sounds sensible. Are there any downsides I should be aware of?

16 · Harin JeonPlatform Framework

The main downside is timing. Atlas is three months ahead of us. If Atlas is in code freeze while we're still adding features, a shared branch can create tension, because they won't want any risky changes. We'll need a clear policy for how Atlas Lite features are merged during Atlas's stabilization period, probably behind feature flags that are turned off for Atlas.

Hardware Constraints

17 · Mark YoonProgram Management

Let's talk about the hardware itself. What's different about Atlas Lite that affects software?

18 · Dongwook KangKernel & BSP

The biggest one is memory. Atlas has twelve gigabytes of RAM. Atlas Lite will ship with six gigabytes in most markets, and four in a few price-sensitive ones. The Atlas software was designed with a lot of headroom. On a four-gigabyte device, some features, like on-device AI or keeping many apps in the background, will behave very differently.

19 · Harin JeonPlatform Framework

That's my biggest concern too. Some of the new Atlas features assume they can keep a large model in memory. On four gigabytes, they'll either have to be disabled or redesigned. I'd like the product team to tell us early which features are mandatory on Atlas Lite, so we don't spend months optimizing something that gets cut anyway.

20 · Mark YoonProgram Management

I'll make that my first action item. I'll ask product management for a feature list with three categories: must have, nice to have, and Atlas only, by the end of August.

21 · Nina ChoSQE

And please ask them to decide on the four-gigabyte variant early as well. Testing two memory configurations doubles a lot of our performance and stability work. If the four-gigabyte version is only for two small markets, I'd want to know whether it's worth that cost.

Draft Milestones

22 · Mark YoonProgram Management

Now the schedule. Here's my draft, working backward from June 4th. Release candidate on April 30th. Code freeze on April 9th. Feature complete on February 26th. First boot on Q7 hardware on October 26th. And development starts when Qorvix delivers the first Q7 BSP, which they've said will be September 28th. Please tear it apart.

23 · Dongwook KangKernel & BSP

First boot four weeks after the BSP drop is reasonable for a chipset family we know. But I'd push for starting before September 28th. Qorvix has an evaluation board for the Q7 that's available now. If we get two of them in early September, we can start porting our shared drivers to 6.12 a month earlier, and first boot on real hardware becomes much less risky.

24 · Mark YoonProgram Management

That sounds like a good investment. Is there any reason not to?

25 · Chris MoonKernel & BSP

Only staffing. Dongwook is free from next week, but the other two kernel engineers for Atlas Lite are on Orion Lite until the September release. So early work would be Dongwook alone, which is fine for porting, but he shouldn't be expected to solve everything by himself.

26 · Nina ChoSQE

My concern is later in the schedule. Atlas code freeze is January 22nd, and its final stability campaign runs into February. Atlas Lite feature complete is February 26th. That means SQE is running the Atlas final campaign and the Atlas Lite pre-feature-complete testing at the same time. We don't have enough test devices or people for both.

27 · Mark YoonProgram Management

What would you suggest?

28 · Nina ChoSQE

Two options. Either we move Atlas Lite feature complete to mid-March, after the Atlas campaign ends, which compresses our own stabilization window. Or we start Atlas Lite stability testing much earlier, in December, while it's still lightweight, and accept that we'll find bugs later than ideal. I lean toward the second option, but only if the shared branch works as Harin described, so fixes found on Atlas automatically benefit Atlas Lite.

29 · Harin JeonPlatform Framework

That's a strong argument for the shared branch. On a shared branch, the Atlas final stability campaign is effectively testing a big part of Atlas Lite's code as well. We'd only need to focus Atlas Lite testing on the device-specific parts and the memory configuration.

Risks and Dependencies

30 · Mark YoonProgram Management

Let me list the risks I've heard so far, and you tell me what I've missed. The Qorvix Q7 BSP date. Memory constraints on the four-gigabyte variant. Staffing until the Orion Lite September release. SQE capacity overlapping with Atlas. And merge policy during Atlas's code freeze.

31 · Chris MoonKernel & BSP

Add one more: Qorvix's own GKI readiness. Their Orion drivers were written for the old non-GKI model. They told us the Q7 BSP will be GKI-compliant from the first drop, but that's a big change for them, and I'd want to see the first drop before I believe it.

32 · Dongwook KangKernel & BSP

Related to that: if the first Q7 drop isn't GKI-compliant, we'll have to do a lot of the conversion work ourselves, which would push first boot out by several weeks. I'd suggest we ask Qorvix to share their symbol list requests with us early, so we can see how much non-standard access their drivers need.

33 · Mark YoonProgram Management

Good. I'll add both to the risk register, with Chris as the owner for the Qorvix GKI risk.

Staffing and Knowledge Transfer

34 · Mark YoonProgram Management

Let me ask a basic question about staffing. How big does the Atlas Lite team need to be, and where are those people coming from? I've been told the headcount is fixed, so I need to understand what we can realistically do with it.

35 · Chris MoonKernel & BSP

For kernel and BSP, the plan is six engineers at full strength: Dongwook plus two from Orion Lite after the September release, and three from Orion after its maintenance load drops in October. That's enough, but only because so much of the Qorvix knowledge already exists within those teams. If we had to hire from outside, it would take months to get people up to speed.

36 · Harin JeonPlatform Framework

On the framework side, it's trickier. Most of the expertise on the new Atlas features is with the Atlas team, and they won't be free until after Atlas launches in March. So for Atlas Lite, we'll mostly be integrating features that someone else designed, and we'll need their help whenever something behaves differently on low memory.

37 · Mark YoonProgram Management

How do we make sure that help is actually available when we need it? Everyone on Atlas will be under pressure in January and February.

38 · Harin JeonPlatform Framework

I'd suggest naming a 'feature buddy' on the Atlas team for each major feature, someone who agrees to spend a few hours a week answering our questions. It needs to be formally agreed with their managers, otherwise it'll be the first thing to disappear when Atlas gets busy.

39 · Dongwook KangKernel & BSP

We could do the same on the kernel side, in the other direction. I learned a lot about Qorvix power management on Pulse Watch, and the Atlas kernel team will hit similar problems on Arvenna. Sharing what we learn across both chipsets should help everyone.

40 · Mark YoonProgram Management

I like that a lot. I'll talk to the Atlas program manager this week and propose the buddy system formally, so it's part of both programs' plans rather than an informal favor.

Quality Strategy

41 · Nina ChoSQE

Before we finish, I'd like to propose one quality principle for Atlas Lite. On Nova Tab, the thermal regression slipped through because our long-duration tests only ran on release candidates. For Atlas Lite, I want sustained performance and memory pressure tests running weekly from first boot, not just at the end. Mid-range devices live and die by how they perform after an hour of use, not in a three-minute benchmark.

42 · Harin JeonPlatform Framework

I'd support that, especially for memory. On low-memory devices, the problems usually show up as apps restarting in the background, which users find extremely annoying but which never appears as a crash in our dashboards.

43 · Mark YoonProgram Management

Then let's make it a program requirement, with its own line in the weekly status report. Nina, could you define the tests and the pass criteria?

44 · Nina ChoSQE

Yes. I'll include an app restart metric: how many of the last ten used apps are still in memory after thirty minutes of mixed use. That's the number users actually feel.

Decisions and Action Items

45 · Mark YoonProgram Management

Let me summarize the decisions. One common framework branch with Atlas, with device configuration on top and feature flags during Atlas's freeze. Shared kernel modules for anything that isn't chipset-specific. Early development on Q7 evaluation boards from early September, with the official start on September 28th. First boot October 26th, feature complete February 26th, code freeze April 9th, release candidate April 30th, launch June 4th. Atlas Lite stability testing starts in December. Any objections?

46 · Chris MoonKernel & BSP

No objection from kernel.

47 · Mark YoonProgram Management

Action items. Dongwook: request two Q7 evaluation boards from Qorvix this week. Chris: get written confirmation of the Q7 BSP date and their GKI readiness, including their symbol list requests. Harin: draft the shared branch and feature flag policy, and review it with the Atlas framework leads. Nina: define the weekly sustained performance and memory tests. And I'll get the feature list from product management by August 31st.

48 · Harin JeonPlatform Framework

I'll set up the review with the Atlas leads for next Thursday. If they have concerns, better to hear them now than in January.

Closing and Next Meeting

49 · Mark YoonProgram Management

Thank you all. For a first meeting, I feel like I learned more in an hour than in a week of reading documents. Our next checkpoint is Sunday, August 30th, at 3 p.m. Korea time, here. By then, we should have the evaluation boards ordered, the feature list, and the branch policy draft.

50 · Dongwook KangKernel & BSP

Sounds good. And Mark, keep asking the basic questions. They're useful.

51 · Mark YoonProgram Management

I'll take you up on that. And I'll send out the minutes tonight with the draft milestones, the risk register, and the action items, so anyone who wasn't here today can catch up before Monday. See you all in two weeks.

52 · Nina ChoSQE

Thanks, Mark. Please include the four-gigabyte question in the minutes too, so product management sees it as an open decision.

참석자 확인 및 소개

안녕하세요, 여러분. 일요일에 나와 주셔서 감사합니다. 대부분의 시간을 일정 논의에 쓰고 싶어서 소개는 빨리 하겠습니다. 저는 프로그램 관리팀 Mark이고, 막 Atlas Lite 소프트웨어 프로그램 매니저로 배정됐습니다. 시스템 소프트웨어 쪽 프로그램은 처음이라, 기초적인 질문을 해도 너그럽게 봐 주세요.

02 · Chris MoonKernel & BSP

환영합니다, Mark. 기초적인 질문이 보통 진짜 문제를 잡아내죠. 저는 커널·BSP 팀 Chris입니다. Orion Lite 업무에 더해 Atlas Lite 커널 리드를 맡게 됩니다.

03 · Dongwook KangKernel & BSP

같은 커널·BSP 팀 강동욱입니다. Pulse Watch 출시를 막 마쳐서, 다음 주부터 Atlas Lite 브링업에 집중할 수 있습니다.

04 · Harin JeonPlatform Framework

플랫폼 프레임워크팀 전하린입니다. Atlas Lite의 프레임워크 작업을 이끌게 됩니다. 저희 팀 대부분이 현재 Atlas에 있어서, 두 프로그램 간에 코드를 어떻게 공유할지에 특히 관심이 있습니다.

SQE 조니나입니다. 품질 리드를 맡게 됩니다. 지난주까지 Nova Tab 출시에 있어서, 프로그램 막바지에 일이 어떻게 틀어지는지 아주 최근에 다시 경험했습니다.

프로그램 배경

06 · Mark YoonProgram Management

배경을 말씀드리겠습니다. Atlas Lite는 Atlas의 중급형 동반 모델입니다. 사업부는 같은 안드로이드 버전과 같은 주요 기능으로 Atlas의 소프트웨어 경험을 공유하되, 더 낮은 가격대를 맞추기 위해 Qorvix의 새 중급 칩셋인 Q7을 쓰길 원합니다. 목표 시장 출시일은 Atlas보다 약 3개월 뒤인 2027년 6월 4일입니다.

07 · Chris MoonKernel & BSP

그리고 바로 그게 핵심 과제입니다. Atlas는 Arvenna, Atlas Lite는 Qorvix입니다. 사용자는 같은 소프트웨어를 보지만, 커널과 BSP 작업은 거의 완전히 별개입니다. 칩셋 벤더가 둘이면 BSP도 둘, 드라이버도 두 벌, 벤더 버그도 두 벌이죠.

08 · Mark YoonProgram Management

제가 이해해야 할 게 바로 그런 부분이네요. Orion과 Orion Lite도 Qorvix인데, Qorvix를 다시 쓰는 데 장점이 있나요?

09 · Dongwook KangKernel & BSP

물론이죠. 저희는 Qorvix 팀, 그들의 릴리스 절차와 도구를 잘 압니다. 디스플레이, 센서, 충전용 Orion 드라이버 다수는 약간만 수정해서 재사용할 수 있습니다. 그리고 Q7은 Orion이 쓰는 칩셋 계열의 원가 절감 버전이라, 하드웨어 블록 상당수가 익숙합니다. 완전히 새로운 벤더로 시작하는 것보다 위험이 낮습니다.

10 · Chris MoonKernel & BSP

그런데 중요한 차이가 하나 있습니다. Orion은 커널 6.1이지만, Atlas Lite는 새 안드로이드 릴리스로 나오는 신규 단말이라 6.12로 출시해야 합니다. 그래서 Orion의 드라이버 로직은 재사용할 수 있지만, Orion의 커널은 재사용할 수 없습니다. 금요일에 소라 님이 발표한 GKI 작업이 바로 여기서 직접 관련됩니다.

소프트웨어 플랫폼 전략

11 · Mark YoonProgram Management

코드를 어떻게 구성할지 이야기해 봅시다. 하린 님, Atlas Lite용 별도 브랜치를 만드나요, 아니면 두 제품이 한 브랜치에 있나요?

12 · Harin JeonPlatform Framework

저는 프레임워크에 대해 공통 브랜치 하나를 두고, 그 위에 단말별 설정을 얹는 방식을 강하게 선호합니다. Orion과 Orion Lite 때 별도 브랜치를 써 봤는데, 수정 사항을 이쪽저쪽으로 포팅하느라 몇 달을 썼습니다. 작년 Orion Lite 시장 이슈의 절반은 Orion에서 이미 고쳤지만 포팅하지 않은 버그였어요.

13 · Nina ChoSQE

품질 쪽에서도 전적으로 동의합니다. 브랜치가 하나면 수정도 한 벌입니다. 하지만 Atlas를 위해 한 변경이 Atlas Lite를 깨뜨릴 수 있고, 그 반대도 마찬가지라는 뜻이기도 합니다. 그 방향으로 간다면, 맨 처음부터 모든 변경에 대해 두 단말 모두에서 자동 테스트가 필요합니다.

14 · Chris MoonKernel & BSP

커널은 상황이 조금 다릅니다. GKI에서는 어차피 핵심 커널이 공유됩니다. 벤더 모듈은 칩셋마다 다르겠지만, 발열 프레임워크나 충전 정책 같은 공통 로직은 두 단말이 함께 쓰는 공유 모듈에 있어야 합니다. 명확한 규칙이 있었으면 합니다. 칩셋에 특화된 코드가 아니면 공유 모듈로 간다는 거죠.

15 · Mark YoonProgram Management

합리적으로 들리네요. 제가 알아야 할 단점이 있나요?

16 · Harin JeonPlatform Framework

주된 단점은 타이밍입니다. Atlas는 저희보다 3개월 앞서 있습니다. 저희가 아직 기능을 추가하는 동안 Atlas가 코드 프리즈에 들어가면, 공유 브랜치에서 긴장이 생길 수 있습니다. 그쪽은 위험한 변경을 원하지 않을 테니까요. Atlas 안정화 기간 동안 Atlas Lite 기능을 어떻게 머지할지 명확한 정책이 필요한데, 아마 Atlas에서는 꺼져 있는 기능 플래그 뒤에 두는 방식이 될 겁니다.

하드웨어 제약

17 · Mark YoonProgram Management

하드웨어 자체 이야기를 해 봅시다. Atlas Lite에서 소프트웨어에 영향을 주는 차이점은 뭔가요?

18 · Dongwook KangKernel & BSP

가장 큰 건 메모리입니다. Atlas는 RAM이 12GB입니다. Atlas Lite는 대부분 시장에서 6GB, 가격에 민감한 일부 시장에서는 4GB로 나옵니다. Atlas 소프트웨어는 여유를 많이 두고 설계됐습니다. 4GB 단말에서는 온디바이스 AI나 많은 앱을 백그라운드에 유지하는 기능 같은 일부 기능이 매우 다르게 동작할 겁니다.

19 · Harin JeonPlatform Framework

그게 제 가장 큰 걱정이기도 합니다. 새 Atlas 기능 일부는 큰 모델을 메모리에 유지할 수 있다고 가정합니다. 4GB에서는 비활성화하거나 다시 설계해야 합니다. 어차피 빠질 기능을 몇 달씩 최적화하지 않도록, 제품팀이 Atlas Lite에서 필수인 기능이 뭔지 일찍 알려 줬으면 합니다.

20 · Mark YoonProgram Management

그걸 제 첫 실행 항목으로 하겠습니다. 8월 말까지 제품 관리팀에 필수, 있으면 좋음, Atlas 전용의 세 범주로 나눈 기능 목록을 요청하겠습니다.

21 · Nina ChoSQE

그리고 4GB 모델에 대한 결정도 일찍 내려 달라고 해 주세요. 메모리 구성 두 가지를 테스트하면 성능과 안정성 작업 상당 부분이 두 배가 됩니다. 4GB 모델이 작은 시장 두 곳만을 위한 거라면, 그만한 비용을 들일 가치가 있는지 알고 싶습니다.
