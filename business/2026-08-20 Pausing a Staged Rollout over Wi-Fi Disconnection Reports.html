Pausing a Staged Rollout over Wi-Fi Disconnection Reports

Wi-Fi 끊김 보고로 단계적 배포를 멈춘 시장 이슈 대응

Orion 유지보수 업데이트를 1% 사용자에게 배포한 지 이틀 만에 Wi-Fi가 반복적으로 끊긴다는 보고가 급증했습니다. 시장 품질, 릴리스 관리, 커넥티비티 소프트웨어, 칩셋 벤더 Qorvix 커넥티비티 팀이 텔레메트리 분석, 배포 중단 판단, 원인과 대응 방안, 사용자 커뮤니케이션을 논의합니다.

I've paused the expansion to ten percent until we understand it.

Hannah, Field Quality. Kyle is with me, and he has the telemetry breakdown.

Minho Seo, Connectivity Software lead. My team owns the Wi-Fi framework and driver integration. Haeun is our Wi-Fi driver expert.

Victor Lin from Qorvix connectivity engineering, in Hsinchu. I support the Wi-Fi chip and firmware on Orion.

Field Signal

06 · Grace JungRelease Management

Kyle, what are we seeing exactly?

07 · Kyle JangField Quality

In the one percent group, user feedback mentioning Wi-Fi went up about six times compared to the same population before the update. Telemetry confirms it: Wi-Fi disconnect events per device per day went from about one to almost nine. Most users say the same thing: the phone keeps dropping off their home Wi-Fi every few minutes and reconnecting. It's not a crash, but it's extremely annoying, and some users switched to mobile data and burned through their data plans.

08 · Hannah OhField Quality

Is it everyone in the one percent group, or a subset?

09 · Kyle JangField Quality

A clear subset. About eighty percent of the extra disconnects come from devices connected to routers from one manufacturer, and specifically to networks configured in a mixed security mode, where the router accepts both older and newer security standards at the same time. That configuration is the default on that manufacturer's popular home routers in Korea and Japan.

10 · Grace JungRelease Management

So even though only one percent of users have the update, if we expanded, a large share of users in Korea and Japan would be affected.

11 · Kyle JangField Quality

Exactly. Based on our router telemetry, roughly fifteen percent of Orion users in those two countries connect to that router family at home. That's why pausing at one percent was the right call.

Technical Analysis

12 · Grace JungRelease Management

Haeun, what do the driver logs show?

13 · Haeun KimConnectivity Software

The pattern is very consistent. The phone is connected normally, then the router starts a routine key refresh, which happens every few minutes on these routers. During that refresh, our device responds with a message the router doesn't accept, the handshake times out, and the router disconnects us. Then we reconnect from scratch, which takes about three seconds, and the cycle repeats at the next key refresh.

14 · Minho SeoConnectivity Software

And this didn't happen on the previous build, which tells us something changed in that exchange. The maintenance update includes a new Wi-Fi firmware from Qorvix. Victor, can you tell us what changed in the firmware's handling of key refresh?

15 · Victor LinQorvix Connectivity Engineering

Yes. The new firmware includes a security hardening change. It strictly enforces a protection feature for management messages whenever the network advertises support for it. In mixed security mode, these routers advertise the feature as optional, but some of their firmware versions don't actually implement it correctly during key refresh. The old firmware was more lenient and simply ignored the inconsistency. The new firmware treats it as an error.

16 · Hannah OhField Quality

So technically, our device is now doing the correct thing, and the routers are the ones that don't follow the standard.

17 · Victor LinQorvix Connectivity Engineering

That's a fair summary from a standards perspective. But I understand that customers don't care who is technically correct. Their Wi-Fi worked yesterday, and today it doesn't.

18 · Minho SeoConnectivity Software

Exactly. And I'd add that other phones work fine with these routers, which means other device makers handle this case more pragmatically. We have to as well.

Why the Lab Missed It

19 · Grace JungRelease Management

How did this get through our testing? We tested this build for weeks.

20 · Haeun KimConnectivity Software

Our Wi-Fi interoperability lab has about sixty router models, including two from this manufacturer. But both of ours run newer router firmware, where the bug in the router is already fixed. The affected customers mostly have routers that haven't been updated in years. In other words, we tested against the routers we wish customers had, not the ones they actually have.

21 · Kyle JangField Quality

We could fix that with telemetry. We know which router models and firmware versions our users connect to most. If the lab mirrored the top fifty combinations from real usage, including old firmware, we'd catch this kind of thing before release.

22 · Minho SeoConnectivity Software

I like that. It means keeping some routers deliberately outdated, which feels strange, but that's what reality looks like.

23 · Victor LinQorvix Connectivity Engineering

On our side, I have to admit that our release testing for this firmware was also done against current router firmware. We assumed customers keep their routers updated, and that assumption was clearly wrong for this market. I'll raise it with our test team, because other customers of ours sell phones in Korea and Japan too.

24 · Hannah OhField Quality

That's appreciated. It also suggests a broader lesson for our release process: any firmware change that touches security behavior should get a staged rollout that starts smaller and stays longer at the first step, because interoperability problems often only appear in the field.

25 · Grace JungRelease Management

Agreed. I'll propose that rule at the next release council: security-related firmware changes start at half a percent and stay there for at least seventy-two hours.

Response Options

26 · Grace JungRelease Management

Let's talk about options. I'm conscious that the one percent group is suffering right now, and that the rest of the update, including the storage fix, is waiting.

27 · Victor LinQorvix Connectivity Engineering

From our side, there are two possible firmware changes. One: revert the strict enforcement entirely. That's quick, but it also removes a security improvement that protects against a known class of attacks. Two: keep strict enforcement in general, but fall back to the lenient behavior when the network advertises the feature as optional and the handshake fails in this specific way. That's safer, but it needs about a week to implement and test.

28 · Haeun KimConnectivity Software

There's also something we can do on the driver side without new firmware. The driver tells the firmware which security features to use when it connects. For networks in mixed mode, we can tell the firmware not to require the protection feature. It has the same effect as the fallback for these routers, and it's a configuration change in our driver, so it could be ready in two days.

29 · Minho SeoConnectivity Software

The downside is that it applies to all mixed-mode networks, not just the buggy routers. Devices on correctly working mixed-mode routers would lose the extra protection too. But that's exactly how the previous build behaved, so we wouldn't be any worse off than before the update.

30 · Grace JungRelease Management

That's an important point. We'd be returning to known, previously shipped behavior, not inventing something new. I like the shape of that as an immediate step, with Victor's smarter firmware fallback as the long-term solution.

31 · Hannah OhField Quality

What about the one percent group right now? Can we help them faster than a new build?

32 · Haeun KimConnectivity Software

Yes, actually. The driver setting I described can also be pushed through our device configuration service, the same way Seojin's team changed the modem restart policy in Spain. If we push it to the one percent group today, they should see relief within hours, without waiting for a new build.

33 · Grace JungRelease Management

That's the best news I've heard today. Let's do that first, on the same cautious pattern: a small slice of the one percent group, watch for a few hours, then all of them.

The Security Trade-off

34 · Hannah OhField Quality

Before we move on, I want to make sure we're comfortable with the security side of the configuration change. We're deliberately turning off a protection on some networks. Should Product Security be part of this decision?

35 · Minho SeoConnectivity Software

Yes, I think they should at least review it. I'll send Jiho a short summary today. My expectation is that he'll be comfortable, because we're only restoring the behavior we've shipped for the last two years on these networks, and the protection still applies on networks that require it rather than offering it as optional.

36 · Victor LinQorvix Connectivity Engineering

For context, the attack this feature protects against requires the attacker to be physically close to the victim's network and to actively interfere with it. It's a real risk, but not something that affects most home users on a daily basis. That's why many vendors are cautious about enforcing it strictly on mixed-mode networks.

37 · Grace JungRelease Management

That's helpful context. But I'd like the long-term solution to restore the protection wherever the router actually supports it correctly. Victor, does your smarter fallback do that?

38 · Victor LinQorvix Connectivity Engineering

Yes. With the firmware fallback, the device tries the protected exchange first, and only if the router fails in this specific way does it fall back for that network. Routers that implement it correctly keep the protection. Once that firmware ships, we can remove the driver configuration entirely.

39 · Haeun KimConnectivity Software

I'll add an expiry note to the configuration, so it's clear it's temporary and tied to Victor's firmware. Otherwise, in a year, nobody will remember why it's there.

Resuming the Rollout

45 · Grace JungRelease Management

46 · Minho SeoConnectivity Software

The configuration service can target devices by build number, so we can set it up to apply to every device that receives the update.

47 · Kyle JangField Quality

I'd want twenty-four hours of clean data from the one percent group after the configuration is applied before we go to ten percent. Wi-Fi problems tend to show up in the evening, when people are home, so one evening of data is the minimum.

Agreed. If tonight's data is clean, we resume to ten percent on Saturday morning, and continue with the original halt criteria plus a new one: Wi-Fi disconnects per device per day must stay within twenty percent of the pre-update baseline.
