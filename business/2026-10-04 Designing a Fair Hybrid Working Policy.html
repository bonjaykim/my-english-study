Designing a Fair Hybrid Working Policy

공정한 하이브리드 근무 정책 협의

가상의 기업이 사무실 참석 기준과 업무 유연성을 협의한다. 동일 출근 횟수와 직무별 형평성의 충돌을 해결하고 협업 목적에 따른 출근, 예외 처리, 결과 기반 평가를 정한다. 모든 인물·회사·수치는 가상이다.

01 / English script

Attendance and policy purpose

01 · AishaPeople Operations

Welcome to our hybrid working discussion on October fourth. I am Aisha from People Operations, and Mina will represent the employee feedback process. Tom joins from Engineering, Yuna from Client Services, and Felix from Workplace Support. This Sunday session is an exceptional planning workshop for our company, with attendance arranged in advance. We need a policy proposal that supports collaboration without equating physical presence with performance. Please confirm that you have the employee survey and the manager comments. We are not deciding individual employment arrangements today. Our task is to identify the default expectations, a fair way to handle exceptions, and evidence we can use to evaluate the policy after it begins.

02 · TomEngineering

I have both documents. Engineering currently uses two shared office days, but the practical value varies. A design review with a physical whiteboard can benefit from being together, while an afternoon of individual code review often does not. My concern is that a fixed attendance quota will encourage people to travel merely to join video calls from different desks. I can support planned in-person collaboration, but I want teams to explain its purpose. We also hire colleagues outside the commuting area. If the policy quietly assumes that everyone lives nearby, those colleagues could become less visible in decisions even though their employment arrangements were accepted before they joined the company.

03 · YunaClient Services

I have the materials too. Client Services has a different constraint because customers sometimes request onsite workshops with little flexibility in timing. Some of our staff already spend three days a week at customer locations. If we add two compulsory office days, their practical flexibility disappears. On the other hand, newer colleagues need access to experienced staff, and fully separate schedules have made coaching difficult. I do not want a policy that looks equal on paper but produces unequal burdens across roles. We need to distinguish customer-site work from internal attendance and ensure that coaching is scheduled deliberately rather than depending on whoever happens to be in the office on a particular afternoon.

04 · FelixWorkplace Support

Workplace Support has the same documents. We can provide room bookings, equipment, and accessibility support, but our capacity is not unlimited. When every team chooses Tuesday, there are more requests for quiet spaces than we can accommodate. When schedules are spread thoughtfully, the office works much better. I would like the policy to include planning notice rather than treating desk availability as someone else's problem. We also need a route for colleagues who require a suitable workspace at home or in the office. Those requests should not be mixed with performance judgments. A person asking for an adapted chair or a quiet room should not have to prove that they deserve flexibility before we discuss practical support.

Employee evidence and the disputed rule

05 · MinaPeople Operations

The survey includes responses from one hundred and twenty employees, representing about three quarters of our workforce. The strongest theme is predictability, not a demand for unlimited remote work. People want to know when they must travel and what collaboration will happen when they arrive. Several respondents said office days are announced the evening before. Others said they miss important discussions because decisions happen informally after a meeting ends. We should be careful with the headline preference for two remote days. Different people meant different things by that answer, and the survey did not distinguish client travel from office attendance. I recommend follow-up conversations before we interpret that number as a mandate for one identical weekly pattern.

06 · AishaPeople Operations

The original draft required three office days every week. Managers supported it because they wanted a simple rule they could explain consistently. I still see the value of clarity, but the examples suggest that equal treatment is not necessarily equitable treatment. Could we replace the quota with two planned collaboration days for teams whose work benefits from regular in-person contact, while allowing client-site work to count toward the planned location requirement? Teams with established remote contracts would need a separate arrangement. I am concerned that too much discretion will lead to manager favoritism, so we would need published criteria rather than simply telling managers to use their judgment.

07 · TomEngineering

I would challenge the assumption that two days should be the default for every team with some in-person work. Our platform team might need one concentrated workshop every other week, while our hardware group needs regular lab access. A default can be useful, but it should not become a disguised minimum that people cannot question. Could each team publish a collaboration charter explaining the activities that require physical presence, the notice period, and how remote colleagues participate? People Operations could review the charters for consistency. That would let us compare the reasons behind different arrangements rather than compare attendance counts that say very little about whether the work is actually coordinated well.

08 · YunaClient Services

I like the charter idea, but we should make sure a team cannot simply write whatever its manager prefers. Client Services could say that customer needs require complete flexibility, leaving employees with no predictable home days at all. We need a company-level floor for notice and schedule stability. I suggest publishing ordinary location plans two weeks ahead and treating shorter notice as an exception that requires a reason. Customer emergencies will still happen, but repeated late requests should trigger a workload review. Otherwise, the exception becomes the operating model. The policy should also explain that employees can raise a schedule conflict without that being recorded as a lack of commitment to customers or colleagues.

Fairness, coaching, and privacy

09 · FelixWorkplace Support

Two weeks of notice will help room planning, and I can provide a weekly capacity view so teams can choose suitable days. However, I do not want us to solve space pressure by asking employees to compete for desks each morning. Teams should reserve the spaces they need when they publish the plan. If a workshop requires accessible equipment, we need to know that early enough to prepare it. For individual support requests, Workplace Support can coordinate practical arrangements with People Operations while keeping personal explanations restricted. Managers need to know the agreed arrangement, not every detail behind it. That boundary will make colleagues more willing to ask for help before a problem becomes disruptive.

10 · MinaPeople Operations

I want to add a concern about proximity bias. Employees described managers praising colleagues they see regularly while overlooking remote contributions that are less visible. A new location policy will not solve that by itself. We need performance discussions to use agreed outcomes, documented collaboration, and feedback from the people affected by the work. Attendance should not be a proxy for contribution. At the same time, remote work should not mean disappearing from shared responsibilities. The charter can specify response expectations during working hours and how decisions are documented. We should hold everyone to those expectations regardless of location, instead of using office presence as an easy substitute for evaluating whether the team is functioning.

11 · TomEngineering

That is fair, and Engineering needs to improve decision documentation. We sometimes resolve a design question at lunch and forget to record why we chose an option. Remote colleagues then receive a conclusion without context and are expected to implement it. I propose a simple rule: any decision that changes shared work must be added to the team record before it is treated as final. Informal conversations can generate ideas, but they should not bypass the people responsible for the outcome. We can also rotate workshop facilitation so the same office-based colleagues do not control every discussion. That would make collaboration more inclusive without pretending that every activity works equally well in every location.

12 · YunaClient Services

For coaching, I would prefer scheduled access rather than a rule that junior employees must be onsite more often. That could make flexibility depend on seniority while leaving the actual coaching inconsistent. We can assign each new colleague a mentor and publish weekly coaching slots, some in person and some online. If a task requires supervised practice, the charter should name it and explain the arrangement. We should also ask newer staff whether the sessions are useful, rather than measure success through the number of meetings alone. A calendar full of coaching appointments can still leave someone confused if the mentor is distracted or the discussion never addresses the problems they are encountering.

Policy proposal and implementation owners

13 · AishaPeople Operations

We have a proposal that is more nuanced than the original quota. Each team will publish a reviewed collaboration charter, ordinary location plans will be available two weeks ahead, and existing remote arrangements will remain explicit rather than being silently overridden. Client-site work will be included in the location plan, and short-notice changes will require a stated business reason. Performance assessment will use contribution criteria, not attendance totals. We will also establish a confidential request route for individual adjustments. Before we implement this policy, we need to resolve any conflicts with current agreements. Our company's authorized reviewers will check the final proposal before implementation, and we will communicate the actual approved wording rather than treat today's discussion as an immediate change to everyone's contract.

14 · MinaPeople Operations

I will draft the employee guidance by Wednesday, October seventh, at noon Asia/Seoul. It will explain how to ask for an individual adjustment, what information is needed, and how a decision can be reviewed if the employee believes the criteria were applied inconsistently. We should not require people to disclose sensitive personal details in a team meeting. I will also run follow-up conversations with ten employees across different roles and locations, including client-facing staff and colleagues outside the commuting area. Their comments will test whether the proposal is understandable and whether any group faces an unintended burden. I will report themes without attributing personal circumstances to named employees in the manager briefing.

15 · FelixWorkplace Support

I will publish the office capacity view and reservation instructions by Thursday, October eighth, at three p.m. Asia/Seoul. The view will show available collaboration rooms, quiet spaces, and accessible equipment without identifying who has requested an adjustment. I will test the booking process with two teams before we make it the standard route. We also need a fallback when a reserved room becomes unavailable. I can provide an alternative space or help move the session online, but teams should receive a clear notice rather than arrive to find no suitable place. The workplace service should support the collaboration plan, not become a hidden obstacle that employees are blamed for failing to navigate.

16 · TomEngineering

Engineering will submit three draft charters by Friday, October ninth, at eleven a.m. Asia/Seoul, covering platform, application, and hardware work. Each will name the activities that benefit from physical presence, the participation route for remote colleagues, and the decision documentation rule. We will not use the same schedule for all three simply because it is easier to announce. I will also brief managers on proximity bias and ask them to review whether their feedback includes evidence from remote work. Yuna and I can compare the coaching provisions so new employees receive predictable support across departments. Differences should follow the nature of the work, not the enthusiasm of an individual manager for seeing everyone at a desk.

Evaluation and the next review

17 · YunaClient Services

Client Services will submit its charter on the same Friday deadline. I will include customer-site planning, mentor availability, and how we handle unavoidable late changes. I would like the evaluation to track how often short-notice changes happen and which customers drive them. That will help us address the planning problem with the customer rather than normalize constant disruption for staff. We should also look at coaching usefulness and whether colleagues understand where decisions are recorded. I will avoid collecting detailed movement data. We need evidence about the working arrangement, not a surveillance system that logs every location change while telling employees that the policy is based on trust and responsible collaboration.

18 · MinaPeople Operations

For the employee feedback, we can use an anonymous pulse survey after four weeks and again after eight weeks. It will ask about schedule predictability, access to support, and participation in decisions. We should report results by broad role groups only where the group size is sufficient to protect anonymity. If a small team has a specific concern, we can handle it through a confidential conversation rather than publish a table that effectively identifies the speaker. I will also ask whether the exception process feels usable. A policy can look fair while an employee believes that making a request will damage their reputation. We need to test that experience, not merely count how many forms were submitted.

19 · AishaPeople Operations

Our next proposal review is Monday, October twelfth, at one p.m. Asia/Seoul, for fifty minutes. We will compare the charters, employee feedback, and workplace capacity before sending the final draft to the authorized reviewers. The intended policy start is November second, allowing time for communication and schedule planning. That date is not permission to skip the review. If a material fairness issue remains unresolved, we will revise the proposal before it takes effect. I will send a decision summary that distinguishes the company-wide expectations from team-specific arrangements. Please check that summary carefully, because people should not have to infer the policy from rumors about what another department has decided to do.

20 · FelixWorkplace Support

That timeline works for Workplace Support. I will make the practical information easy to find and include a named contact for booking difficulties. If teams see a capacity conflict, they should raise it during planning rather than tell employees to improvise after they arrive. We have moved away from a simple attendance target toward purposeful collaboration with predictable arrangements. That does not eliminate every tension, but it gives us a way to examine the tensions openly. I appreciate that the evaluation will include the employee experience as well as manager observations. With both perspectives, we can adjust the policy based on how work is actually happening rather than defend a rule solely because it sounded clear in the first draft.

참석 확인과 정책 목적

Engineering의 Tom, Client Services의 Yuna, Workplace Support의 Felix가 참석합니다. 우리 회사의 예외적인 일요일 워크숍이며 참석을 미리 조율했습니다. 물리적 출석을 성과와 동일시하지 않으며 협업을 지원하는 정책 제안이 필요합니다. 설문과 관리자 의견을 확인해 주세요. 개인 근무 계약을 결정하는 자리가 아닙니다. 기본 기대, 공정한 예외 처리, 시작 후 평가할 증거를 정합니다.

02 · TomEngineering

두 문서가 있습니다. Engineering은 공통 출근일 두 날을 쓰지만 가치는 다릅니다. 실제 화이트보드 설계 검토는 함께하면 좋지만 개인 코드 검토 오후는 그렇지 않을 때가 많습니다. 고정 출석 할당은 서로 다른 책상에서 영상 통화하려고 이동하게 만들까 걱정입니다. 계획된 대면 협업에는 찬성하지만 목적을 설명해야 합니다. 통근권 밖 동료도 채용합니다. 모두 근처에 산다고 정책이 가정하면 입사 전 근무 방식을 수락했어도 결정에서 덜 보일 수 있습니다.

03 · YunaClient Services

저도 자료가 있습니다. Client Services는 고객이 시간 조정이 어려운 현장 워크숍을 요청합니다. 일부 직원은 이미 주 3일 고객 현장에 있습니다. 의무 사무실 2일을 더하면 유연성이 사라집니다. 반면 신입은 경험 많은 동료 접근이 필요하고 완전히 다른 일정은 코칭을 어렵게 했습니다. 종이상 동일하지만 직무별 부담이 다른 정책을 원하지 않습니다. 고객 현장과 내부 출석을 구분하고 특정 오후에 우연히 출근한 사람에 의존하지 않으며 코칭을 계획해야 합니다.

04 · FelixWorkplace Support

Workplace Support도 같은 자료가 있습니다. 공간 예약, 장비, 접근성 지원은 가능하지만 무한하지 않습니다. 모두 화요일을 고르면 조용한 공간 요청이 수용 능력을 넘습니다. 일정을 나누면 더 잘 작동합니다. 책상 가용성을 남의 문제로 보지 않고 사전 계획 통지를 정책에 넣었으면 합니다. 집이나 사무실에서 적절한 공간이 필요한 동료의 요청 경로도 필요합니다. 성과 판단과 섞어서는 안 됩니다. 맞춤 의자나 조용한 방을 원하는 사람이 지원 논의 전 유연성을 받을 자격을 입증해야 해서는 안 됩니다.

직원 의견과 논란이 되는 규칙

05 · MinaPeople Operations

우리 직원 약 4분의 3인 120명이 응답했습니다. 핵심은 무제한 원격 요구가 아니라 예측 가능성입니다. 이동 시점과 도착 후 협업을 알고 싶어 합니다. 전날 저녁 출근 공지가 있고 회의 뒤 비공식 결정 때문에 중요한 논의를 놓친다는 답도 있습니다. 원격 이틀 선호라는 요약을 주의해야 합니다. 의미가 다르고 설문은 고객 이동과 사무실 출근을 구분하지 않았습니다. 같은 주간 패턴에 대한 명령으로 해석하기 전에 후속 대화를 권합니다.

06 · AishaPeople Operations

초안은 주 3일 출근이었고 관리자는 일관되게 설명할 단순 규칙이라 지지했습니다. 명확성의 가치는 있지만 동일 대우가 공평한 대우는 아닙니다. 정기 대면이 유익한 팀에는 계획 협업 2일로 바꾸고 고객 현장도 계획 장소 요건으로 인정할 수 있나요? 기존 원격 계약 팀에는 별도 방식이 필요합니다. 지나친 재량은 편애를 만들까 걱정이므로 관리자 판단만 하라고 하지 말고 공개 기준이 필요합니다.

07 · TomEngineering

일부 대면이 있다는 이유로 모든 팀에 이틀을 기본으로 하는 전제에 이의를 제기합니다. 플랫폼팀은 격주 집중 워크숍 하나면 될 수 있고 하드웨어팀은 정기 실험실 접근이 필요합니다. 기본은 유용하지만 질문할 수 없는 숨은 최저 기준이 되어서는 안 됩니다. 팀이 물리적 참석 활동, 통지 기간, 원격 참여를 설명하는 협업 원칙을 공개하면 어떨까요? People Operations가 일관성을 검토합니다. 조율 품질을 거의 말해 주지 않는 출석 수 대신 다른 방식의 이유를 비교할 수 있습니다.

08 · YunaClient Services

원칙은 좋지만 관리자 선호를 마음대로 쓰게 해서는 안 됩니다. Client Services가 고객 필요상 완전 유연성을 주장하면 예측 가능한 재택일이 없어집니다. 통지와 안정성의 회사 차원 최소 기준이 필요합니다. 일반 장소 계획은 2주 전에 공개하고 짧은 통지는 이유 있는 예외로 합시다. 고객 긴급은 생기지만 반복되면 업무량을 검토해야 합니다. 아니면 예외가 운영 모델이 됩니다. 일정 충돌 제기를 고객이나 동료에 대한 헌신 부족으로 기록하지 않는다고 설명해야 합니다.

형평성과 코칭 및 개인정보

2주 통지는 공간 계획에 도움이 됩니다. 주간 수용 현황을 제공해 적절한 날을 고르게 하겠습니다. 하지만 매일 아침 직원이 책상 경쟁을 하게 해 공간 압박을 해결하고 싶지는 않습니다. 계획 공개 때 공간을 예약해야 합니다. 접근성 장비는 준비할 시간을 주어야 합니다. 개인 지원은 설명을 제한하면서 People Operations와 조율할 수 있습니다. 관리자에게는 합의 방식이 필요하지 모든 배경은 필요하지 않습니다. 문제가 커지기 전 도움을 요청하기 쉬워집니다.

근접 편향도 우려입니다. 관리자가 자주 보는 동료를 칭찬하고 덜 보이는 원격 기여를 놓친다는 의견이 있습니다. 장소 정책만으로 해결하지 못합니다. 성과 논의에는 합의 결과, 기록된 협업, 업무 영향받는 사람의 의견을 써야 합니다. 출석은 기여의 대리지표가 되어서는 안 됩니다. 원격도 공동 책임에서 사라지는 것은 아닙니다. 원칙에 근무 중 응답 기대와 결정 기록을 명시할 수 있습니다. 팀 작동 평가 대신 출근을 쉬운 대체물로 쓰지 말고 장소와 무관하게 같은 기대를 적용해야 합니다.

03 / Business expressions

attendance quota

출석 할당 · 출근 횟수 기준을 논의할 때

My concern is that a fixed attendance quota will encourage people to travel merely to join video calls from different desks.

응용 연습
An attendance quota may not improve coordination.

equitable treatment

공평한 대우 · 직무별 부담 차이를 고려할 때

I still see the value of clarity, but the examples suggest that equal treatment is not necessarily equitable treatment.

응용 연습
Equitable treatment considers different work constraints.

collaboration charter

협업 원칙 · 팀별 협업 방식을 명시할 때

Could each team publish a collaboration charter explaining the activities that require physical presence, the notice period, and how remote colleagues participate?

응용 연습
Our collaboration charter explains remote participation.

company-level floor

회사 차원의 최소 기준 · 공통 보호 기준을 정할 때

We need a company-level floor for notice and schedule stability.

응용 연습
The company-level floor requires advance notice.

proximity bias

근접 편향 · 보이는 직원의 평가 우대를 경계할 때

I want to add a concern about proximity bias.

응용 연습
Training can help managers recognize proximity bias.

proxy for contribution

기여의 대리지표 · 출석으로 성과를 대체하지 않게 할 때

Attendance should not be a proxy for contribution.

응용 연습
Online status is a poor proxy for contribution.

scheduled access

예약된 접근 · 코칭을 우연에 의존하지 않게 할 때

For coaching, I would prefer scheduled access rather than a rule that junior employees must be onsite more often.

응용 연습
New hires need scheduled access to mentors.

confidential request route

비공개 요청 경로 · 개인 조정을 신청할 때

We will also establish a confidential request route for individual adjustments.

응용 연습
Provide a confidential request route for support.

participation route

참여 경로 · 원격 동료의 참여 방식을 정할 때

Each will name the activities that benefit from physical presence, the participation route for remote colleagues, and the decision documentation rule.

응용 연습
Every workshop needs a participation route for remote staff.

unavoidable late changes

피할 수 없는 직전 변경 · 긴급 일정 수정을 다룰 때

I will include customer-site planning, mentor availability, and how we handle unavoidable late changes.

응용 연습
Record the reasons for unavoidable late changes.

pulse survey

간단한 주기 설문 · 정책 경험을 추적할 때

For the employee feedback, we can use an anonymous pulse survey after four weeks and again after eight weeks.

응용 연습
A pulse survey can test schedule predictability.

protect anonymity

익명성을 보호하다 · 작은 집단 결과 공개를 판단할 때

We should report results by broad role groups only where the group size is sufficient to protect anonymity.

응용 연습
Combine small groups to protect anonymity.

material fairness issue

중요한 형평성 문제 · 시행 전 해결할 문제를 정할 때

If a material fairness issue remains unresolved, we will revise the proposal before it takes effect.

응용 연습
A material fairness issue requires further review.

purposeful collaboration

목적 있는 협업 · 출석 대신 협업 가치를 설명할 때

We have moved away from a simple attendance target toward purposeful collaboration with predictable arrangements.

응용 연습
The office should support purposeful collaboration.
