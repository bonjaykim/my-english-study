Welcome and agenda

01 · ClaireProcurement

Good morning, everyone. I am Claire from Procurement, and I will lead today's discussion with our packaging supplier. We have invited Operations and Finance because the proposed price increase affects both production planning and our budget. Before we discuss numbers, let us confirm attendance and the purpose of the meeting. We are looking for a workable agreement for the next quarter, not simply the lowest possible price. The outcome needs to protect supply continuity, give us predictable costs, and be realistic for both companies. Please introduce yourselves briefly, including the decisions you are authorized to make today.

02 · EricOperations

Eric here from Operations. I manage production planning at our two assembly sites. I can confirm the delivery schedule and the volume forecast, but I cannot commit to purchasing more material than we expect to use. Our main concern is continuity. We have enough packaging for three weeks at the current production rate, and we need the next shipment to arrive before that stock runs out. A lower price would help, but it would not compensate for a stopped production line. I would like to understand whether the supplier's lead time has changed as well as the price.

03 · NinaFinance

I am Nina from Finance. I can approve changes within the existing quarterly purchasing budget, although a longer contract would need our director's approval. I have reviewed the supplier's request for a twelve percent increase. On our expected volume, that would add approximately twenty-four thousand dollars to the quarter's cost, before any changes in freight. I am here to understand the assumptions behind that request and help compare alternatives fairly. It is important that we do not move a cost from one line of the budget to another and call it a saving.

04 · LeoSupplier

Thank you for including us. I am Leo, the account manager at the supplier. I can discuss pricing options and reserve production capacity, but any exception to our standard payment terms requires approval from our finance team. The increase is driven mainly by raw material costs and a new energy surcharge from our manufacturing partner. We value the relationship and do not want to surprise you with an unexplained number. I have brought a breakdown of the increase and two alternative proposals. I hope we can find a middle ground that works without either side taking on unreasonable risk.

Cost breakdown and requirements

05 · ClaireProcurement

Let us start with the cost breakdown. Our current quarterly spend on packaging is two hundred thousand dollars. A twelve percent increase would take it to two hundred and twenty-four thousand, assuming the same volume and excluding freight. Leo, could you distinguish between costs that are temporary and costs that you expect to remain throughout the quarter? We also need to know when the new price would take effect. Our existing purchase order was accepted last week, so we expect that order to remain at the agreed price. Future orders are the issue we are discussing today.

06 · LeoSupplier

Yes, the accepted purchase order will remain at its original price. The new proposal applies only to orders placed from November first. Roughly eight percentage points of the increase relate to raw materials, and four relate to energy. The energy surcharge is reviewed monthly, so it might come down, but I cannot promise that it will. Rather than present the full twelve percent as permanent, we could separate the base price from the surcharge. That would make the monthly adjustment visible. However, I understand that a variable surcharge makes your budgeting less predictable, which is why our initial proposal used a fixed quarterly price.

07 · NinaFinance

That explanation helps. I would be open to a transparent adjustment mechanism if it has a cap and a clear reference point. An open-ended surcharge is difficult to approve because it transfers almost all the uncertainty to us. Could the contract identify the source of the energy index, specify the review dates, and apply decreases as well as increases? We would also need reasonable notice before any change takes effect. If those details are absent, a seemingly smaller headline increase could turn out to be more expensive than the fixed proposal. I would prefer to compare the likely total cost, not only the first invoice.

08 · EricOperations

From the production side, the delivery pattern is just as important. We currently receive smaller shipments every two weeks because our storage space is limited. If one of the alternatives requires us to accept the entire quarterly volume at once, we would have to rent additional space and move material between locations. That creates handling costs and increases the risk of damage. Please include those operational effects in the comparison. I can consider a slightly larger shipment, but only if it fits within the safe storage limit at each site and does not block access to the production area.

Negotiation and alternatives

09 · LeoSupplier

The first alternative is a nine percent fixed increase for the quarter if you provide a firm monthly forecast and place each order four weeks ahead of delivery. The second is a seven percent base increase plus the variable energy surcharge, capped at five percentage points. Under the second option, your total increase could range from seven to twelve percent. Neither option requires you to store the full quarterly volume. We would hold the material and keep the two-week delivery pattern. What we need in return is better visibility into demand, because last-minute changes make our production schedule significantly more expensive.

10 · ClaireProcurement

The fixed nine percent option is closer to something we could accept, but I want to understand what you mean by a firm forecast. A forecast is our best estimate, whereas a purchase order is a commitment. We should not accidentally turn every forecast update into an obligation to buy unused packaging. Could we agree on a committed volume for the first four weeks and an indicative forecast for the following eight weeks? That would give you a stable near-term production window while preserving some flexibility if our customer orders change later in the quarter.

11 · LeoSupplier

That distinction is fair. We could work with a rolling four-week commitment and an eight-week forecast, provided changes inside the committed window are limited. For example, we could allow a ten percent adjustment in delivery quantity if you give us ten working days of notice. Larger reductions would be difficult because the material would already be in production. I would need our planning manager to confirm those numbers, but the structure is familiar to us. It would be more helpful than the current arrangement, where changes sometimes arrive only a few days before a shipment is due to leave.

12 · EricOperations

I can work with that structure if the adjustment can move in either direction and does not change the agreed delivery date. We sometimes need ten percent more rather than ten percent less. I would also like us to clarify what happens when the supplier misses a shipment. Our volume commitment should not prevent us from buying emergency stock elsewhere if you cannot deliver. We are not looking to penalize every minor delay, but we need a practical exception that protects the production line. Could we include an escalation step before any emergency purchase, with a response deadline from your team?

13 · NinaFinance

Before we add more conditions, let me check the financial comparison. A nine percent fixed increase would add eighteen thousand dollars to our expected quarterly spend. The variable option would add between fourteen thousand and twenty-four thousand, assuming the current volume. The possible saving is four thousand dollars at the low end, but the downside is six thousand above the fixed option. Given our limited budget headroom, I lean toward the fixed price. However, I would like to know whether a change in payment terms could reduce the nine percent without requiring more stock or a longer contract.

14 · LeoSupplier

We could potentially offer an eight percent fixed increase if payment moves from sixty days to thirty days after the invoice date. Earlier payment reduces our financing costs, so that is a real benefit rather than a cosmetic discount. I cannot approve that exception myself, but I can request it from our finance team today. We would still need the rolling forecast arrangement. I want to be transparent that the two concessions are linked: earlier payment alone would not justify the eight percent offer if our production team still faces frequent last-minute volume changes.

15 · NinaFinance

Moving to thirty-day payment is possible, but I need to check the cash-flow impact before agreeing. On the expected spend, an eight percent increase adds sixteen thousand dollars to the quarter, which is two thousand less than the nine percent option. That saving may be worth pursuing, but it comes with earlier cash outflow. I will compare it with our planned payments and confirm whether we can accommodate it. Please also specify that the thirty-day period starts from receipt of a correct invoice, so an invoice error does not reduce the time available for a proper payment review.

16 · ClaireProcurement

That seems worth evaluating. We have not agreed to the eight percent option yet, but we have a clear proposal to take back to our teams. Leo, could you put both fixed-price options in writing, including the forecasting requirements and the proposed payment terms? We also need the delivery exception Eric raised. I suggest a four-hour response target for urgent supply problems during working hours, followed by an agreed recovery plan. If the recovery plan cannot protect production, we should be able to source the affected quantity elsewhere without breaching our commitment to you.

17 · LeoSupplier

I can include that proposal, although the response target will need our operations team's confirmation. I would distinguish between acknowledging an urgent issue and resolving it within four hours. We can realistically acknowledge the issue, assign an owner, and provide an initial recovery estimate in that time. We cannot promise to manufacture and deliver replacement material in four hours. If we keep those expectations separate, the clause will be much more useful. I also propose that the emergency sourcing exception applies only to the quantity we cannot deliver within the agreed recovery window, rather than the entire quarterly commitment.

18 · EricOperations

I agree with that distinction. We need a timely response and a credible recovery plan, not an impossible transport promise. The exception can be limited to the affected quantity. I will provide our storage limits and the latest production forecast by Monday morning. To make the forecast reliable, I will also identify one contact at each assembly site and set a weekly update schedule. That should reduce conflicting messages from different people. If we are asking you to reserve capacity based on our forecast, we have a responsibility to send one consolidated version rather than several spreadsheets that disagree.

Agreement boundaries and responsibilities

19 · ClaireProcurement

Let us capture the agreement in principle, while being precise about what still needs approval. We prefer a fixed quarterly price and the existing two-week delivery pattern. We will evaluate a nine percent increase under current payment terms and an eight percent increase under thirty-day terms. Both would use a rolling four-week commitment and an eight-week indicative forecast. Quantity adjustments of up to ten percent with ten working days of notice are proposed, subject to planning approval. No new commercial terms become binding until both companies approve the written amendment. Is that an accurate summary of the discussion so far?

20 · LeoSupplier

Yes, with one clarification about the dates. The revised pricing would begin with new orders placed on or after November first, not with deliveries arriving after that date. The purchase order already accepted last week remains unchanged. I will send the written options by Monday, October twelfth, at two p.m. Korea time, and I will clearly label any terms that are still awaiting internal approval. I will also include the cost breakdown we reviewed today. That should allow your Finance team to evaluate the proposal without relying on a verbal explanation that may be interpreted differently later.

21 · NinaFinance

I will complete the cash-flow comparison by Tuesday, October thirteenth, at noon Korea time. My recommendation will show the quarterly cost difference and the effect of paying thirty days earlier. I will also confirm which approval is needed for the contract amendment. Please do not issue a revised purchase order based on the eight percent proposal before that review is complete. If earlier payment creates an unacceptable cash constraint, we can still consider the nine percent option. Having a fallback option should help us avoid turning a useful discount discussion into an unnecessary threat to supply continuity.

22 · EricOperations

I will own the operating assumptions, including storage capacity, the rolling forecast, and the escalation contacts. Our first consolidated forecast will be ready by Monday at ten a.m. Korea time, so Leo can use it before completing the written proposal. I will separate confirmed customer orders from expected demand and mark the assumptions behind any uncertain volume. We should also schedule a brief review after the first month of the new arrangement. If the forecasts are consistently inaccurate, we will need to improve the process rather than simply argue about whether the ten percent adjustment allowance is enough.

23 · ClaireProcurement

I will own the comparison sheet and coordinate the contract review. I will make sure that we compare the total cost of ownership, including freight, storage, financing, and any emergency handling costs that can reasonably be estimated. If an item is uncertain, we will show it as an assumption rather than hide it in a single total. Our purchasing decision will consider reliability as well as price. Leo, we appreciate your willingness to explain the cost drivers. In return, we will give you a timely answer once the necessary reviews are finished, rather than leave the proposal sitting without feedback.

Next meeting and closing

24 · LeoSupplier

That approach works for us. I will check the adjustment allowance and the four-hour acknowledgement target with our planning and operations teams today. If either point needs a different number, I will explain the operational reason and propose an alternative rather than simply reject the clause. I will also ask Finance to review the thirty-day payment option. One request from our side: please keep the demand forecast confidential within the teams who need it for planning. We will treat your customer-level information as confidential and use it only to prepare capacity and delivery schedules for this agreement.

25 · ClaireProcurement

Understood. Let us reconvene on Tuesday, October thirteenth, at three p.m. Korea time for twenty minutes. By then we should have the supplier's written options, the consolidated forecast, and Nina's cash-flow review. The purpose of that meeting will be to select a preferred option and identify any final contract wording that needs approval. It will not be a substitute for the written amendment. If an internal review is delayed, please let the group know before Tuesday morning so we can adjust the agenda and avoid asking everyone to attend a meeting without the information needed to decide.

26 · NinaFinance

Tuesday at three is fine for Finance. I will share the comparison before noon, with a short explanation of any assumptions that could change the conclusion. If the eight percent option is affordable, I will recommend it only if the written terms match what we discussed today. A lower number by itself is not enough. The invoice timing, delivery flexibility, and emergency sourcing exception all affect the value of the agreement. I will make those conditions explicit so that the person approving the amendment can see both the benefit and the obligations that come with it.

27 · ClaireProcurement

Thank you, everyone. We have narrowed the discussion to two fixed-price options, clarified the distinction between forecasts and commitments, and assigned the next steps. The current accepted order remains protected at its original price, so there is no need to interrupt the immediate shipment. I will send a written recap today and note which terms remain subject to approval. Please correct any misunderstanding in that recap promptly. Otherwise, we will use it as the basis for Tuesday's decision meeting. Leo, thank you for working through the details with us. We look forward to reviewing the written proposal next week.

초대해 주셔서 감사합니다. 공급업체의 고객 담당자인 Leo입니다. 가격 대안을 논의하고 생산 능력을 확보할 수 있지만 표준 결제 조건의 예외는 저희 재무팀 승인이 필요합니다. 인상은 주로 원자재 비용과 제조 협력사의 새 에너지 할증료 때문입니다. 관계를 소중하게 생각하며 설명 없이 숫자만 제시해 놀라게 하고 싶지는 않습니다. 인상 내역과 대안 두 가지를 준비했습니다. 어느 쪽에도 과도한 위험을 지우지 않으면서 서로 수용할 수 있는 절충점을 찾기를 바랍니다.

비용 내역과 요구사항

05 · ClaireProcurement

비용 내역부터 보겠습니다. 현재 분기 포장재 지출은 20만 달러입니다. 같은 물량에 운송비를 제외하면 12% 인상으로 22만 4천 달러가 됩니다. Leo, 일시적인 비용과 분기 전체에 걸쳐 유지될 것으로 예상하는 비용을 구분해 주시겠어요? 새 가격이 언제부터 적용되는지도 알아야 합니다. 기존 발주서는 지난주 수락됐으므로 그 주문에는 합의한 가격이 유지될 것으로 기대합니다. 오늘 논의하는 것은 이후 주문입니다.

06 · LeoSupplier

네, 수락된 발주서는 원래 가격을 유지합니다. 새 제안은 11월 1일부터 접수되는 주문에만 적용됩니다. 인상분 중 약 8%포인트는 원자재, 4%포인트는 에너지에 해당합니다. 에너지 할증료는 매월 검토하므로 낮아질 수도 있지만 약속할 수는 없습니다. 전체 12%를 영구적인 것으로 제시하는 대신 기본 가격과 할증료를 분리할 수 있습니다. 그러면 월별 조정이 드러납니다. 다만 변동 할증료가 예산 예측을 어렵게 한다는 점을 이해하므로 최초 제안에서는 분기 고정 가격을 사용했습니다.

설명이 도움이 됩니다. 상한과 명확한 기준점이 있다면 투명한 조정 방식에 열려 있습니다. 상한 없는 할증료는 거의 모든 불확실성을 저희에게 넘기므로 승인하기 어렵습니다. 계약에 에너지 지표의 출처와 검토 날짜를 명시하고 인상뿐 아니라 인하도 적용할 수 있나요? 변경 전에 충분한 사전 통지도 필요합니다. 그런 세부사항이 없으면 표면적인 인상률이 작아도 고정 가격보다 더 비싸질 수 있습니다. 첫 청구서뿐 아니라 예상 총비용을 비교하고 싶습니다.

03 / Business expressions

a middle ground

절충점 · 양측이 수용할 수 있는 대안을 찾을 때

I hope we can find a middle ground that works without either side taking on unreasonable risk.

응용 연습
We need to find a middle ground on the delivery schedule.

cost breakdown

비용 내역 · 가격의 구성 요소를 설명하거나 요청할 때

Let us start with the cost breakdown.

응용 연습
Please send a cost breakdown before our review.

take effect

효력이 발생하다 · 새 가격이나 규정의 적용 시점을 확인할 때

We also need to know when the new price would take effect.

응용 연습
The revised terms will take effect next month.

open-ended surcharge

상한 없는 할증료 · 비용 위험이 제한되지 않은 조건을 지적할 때

An open-ended surcharge is difficult to approve because it transfers almost all the uncertainty to us.

응용 연습
We cannot approve an open-ended surcharge.

in return

그 대가로 · 상대에게 제공할 이점과 요구사항을 연결할 때

What we need in return is better visibility into demand, because last-minute changes make our production schedule significantly more expensive.

응용 연습
We can offer a forecast in return for a fixed price.

preserving some flexibility

일부 유연성을 유지하면서 · 확약과 변경 가능성 사이에서 균형을 잡을 때

That would give you a stable near-term production window while preserving some flexibility if our customer orders change later in the quarter.

응용 연습
We want a stable plan while preserving some flexibility.

subject to

~을 조건으로 · 승인이나 검토를 전제로 한 제안을 명시할 때

Quantity adjustments of up to ten percent with ten working days of notice are proposed, subject to planning approval.

응용 연습
The discount is subject to final approval.

cash-flow impact

현금 흐름에 미치는 영향 · 결제 시점 변경의 재무 효과를 검토할 때

Moving to thirty-day payment is possible, but I need to check the cash-flow impact before agreeing.

응용 연습
We should assess the cash-flow impact of advance payment.

in writing

서면으로 · 구두 논의를 문서화하도록 요청할 때

Leo, could you put both fixed-price options in writing, including the forecasting requirements and the proposed payment terms?

응용 연습
Could you confirm the delivery dates in writing?

agreement in principle

원칙적인 합의 · 최종 승인 전 합의된 방향을 정리할 때

Let us capture the agreement in principle, while being precise about what still needs approval.

응용 연습
We have an agreement in principle on the scope.

total cost of ownership

총소유비용 · 구매 가격 외에 운영과 유지 비용도 비교할 때

I will make sure that we compare the total cost of ownership, including freight, storage, financing, and any emergency handling costs that can reasonably be estimated.

응용 연습
The total cost of ownership includes storage and maintenance.

reconvene

다시 모이다 · 후속 회의를 잡으며 필요한 준비를 요청할 때

Let us reconvene on Tuesday, October thirteenth, at three p.m.

응용 연습
Let us reconvene after the legal review.

timely answer

적시에 하는 답변 · 업무 지연을 막을 회신을 약속할 때

In return, we will give you a timely answer once the necessary reviews are finished, rather than leave the proposal sitting without feedback.

응용 연습
We will provide a timely answer to your proposal.

rolling four-week commitment

계속 갱신하는 4주 확약 · 확정 기간과 예상 기간을 구분해 수요를 관리할 때

We could work with a rolling four-week commitment and an eight-week forecast, provided changes inside the committed window are limited.

응용 연습
A rolling four-week commitment gives the factory greater certainty.
