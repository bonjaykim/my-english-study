Running a Blameless Postmortem of the Orion Storage Panic

Orion 스토리지 패닉에 대한 비난 없는 포스트모템

금요 기술 공유 네 번째 세션으로, 이번 달 Orion 유지보수 릴리스를 보류시킨 UFS 커널 패닉에 대해 비난 없는 포스트모템을 진행합니다. 커널·BSP, SQE, 릴리스 관리, 플랫폼 프레임워크 팀이 사건 타임라인, 기여 요인, 잘된 점과 운이 좋았던 점, 비난 없는 문화와 책임의 차이, 재발 방지 조치의 효과 측정을 토론합니다.

01 / English script

Welcome and Ground Rules

01 · Daniel ChoKernel & BSP

Welcome, everyone, to the fourth Friday tech share. Today is a bit different. Instead of a technique, we're sharing a process: the postmortem. Jiwoo will walk us through the storage panic that held the Orion maintenance release earlier this month. Before we start, I want to set the ground rules, because they're what make a postmortem useful.

02 · Daniel ChoKernel & BSP

Rule one: we assume everyone involved made the best decision they could with the information they had at the time. Rule two: we talk about systems and processes, not about individuals. Rule three: if you hear something that sounds like blame, including about yourself, please call it out. The goal is to learn, not to find someone to punish.

03 · Jiwoo LeeKernel & BSP

Thank you, Daniel. I'll admit I was a little nervous about presenting this, because some of the decisions involved were made by people in this room, including Hyunsoo and me. But I think that's exactly why it's worth doing.

04 · Hyunsoo BaeKernel & BSP

And for the record, I'm the one who proposed the shorter timeout. I'm happy to be the example, as long as we keep rule two in mind.

05 · Minji SeoSQE

Minji from SQE. I'll add the testing perspective, since SQE is the team that found it.

06 · Grace JungRelease Management

Grace from Release Management. I'll cover the release decisions, including the hold.

07 · Rachel KimPlatform Framework

Rachel from Framework. I wasn't involved at all, which is why I'm here: I want to learn how to run one of these for my own team.

The Timeline

08 · Jiwoo LeeKernel & BSP

Let's start with the timeline, because a postmortem without a precise timeline turns into a debate about memories. On June 30th, Hyunsoo merged a change that shortened the storage suspend timeout from five hundred to two hundred milliseconds, to save standby power. It was tested with the storage firmware in use at the time, and it worked well.

09 · Jiwoo LeeKernel & BSP

On July 7th, a new storage firmware from Kestrel was integrated by a different engineer, as part of a routine vendor update. It enabled a larger write buffer. That firmware was tested with the standard storage functional tests, which passed. On July 28th, the release candidate containing both changes went to the SQE stress lab. On August 1st, SQE found eleven panics in two hundred devices. On August 3rd, we held the triage meeting and put the release on hold.

10 · Jiwoo LeeKernel & BSP

From August 3rd to 10th, we ran the four-group experiment, confirmed the race condition, and validated the fix. On August 11th, we approved the replacement build, and the staged rollout started on August 18th. Total time from detection to fix approval: ten days. Total delay to the original release: twelve days.

11 · Rachel KimPlatform Framework

Can I ask a clarifying question? Between July 7th and July 28th, did the two changes ever run together anywhere before the release candidate?

12 · Minji SeoSQE

They did, in the nightly builds, but only with short functional tests, which take about an hour per device. The panic needed sustained write traffic combined with screen-off events, and it took an average of six hours to appear even on a stressed device. So no nightly test ran long enough to see it.

Contributing Factors

13 · Jiwoo LeeKernel & BSP

Now the contributing factors. I deliberately say 'factors', plural, because there's almost never a single root cause in incidents like this. I found five. First, the driver had a latent bug: the completion path didn't check whether a request was still valid. That bug had existed for at least two years.

14 · Jiwoo LeeKernel & BSP

Second, the timeout change made the error handler run far more often, which turned a rare race into a common one. Third, the firmware change made flushes take longer, which made the timeout fire more often still. Fourth, the two changes were owned by different engineers and tested separately, so nobody tested the combination. Fifth, our stress testing only ran on release candidates, so the combination wasn't stress-tested until three weeks after it existed.

15 · Daniel ChoKernel & BSP

Notice what's not on the list: 'Hyunsoo shortened the timeout'. That's an event, not a cause. The cause is that our process allowed a timing change to be validated against one firmware and shipped with another.

16 · Hyunsoo BaeKernel & BSP

I appreciate that framing. Honestly, when I made the change, I tested it carefully. I measured flush times on fifty devices and chose a value with more than double the margin. The problem was that the margin was based on a firmware that was about to change, and I had no way of knowing that.

17 · Rachel KimPlatform Framework

That's a really useful point for me. In a blame-focused review, the conversation would stop at 'the timeout was too short'. Here, it continues to 'why didn't the person making the change know the firmware was changing', which leads to a fix that actually prevents the next one.

What Went Well

18 · Jiwoo LeeKernel & BSP

A good postmortem doesn't only look at what went wrong. Several things went very well, and we should keep doing them. The stress lab caught the problem before a single customer saw it. That's the system working as designed.

19 · Minji SeoSQE

The four-group experiment was also a highlight. Instead of arguing about whose change caused it, we designed an experiment that answered the question with data in three days. Having a control group made the results impossible to argue with.

20 · Grace JungRelease Management

From a release perspective, the hold decision was fast and uncontroversial. SQE recommended it, nobody pushed back, and we communicated a realistic new date once, instead of moving it three times. That's a big improvement over how we handled holds two years ago.

21 · Jiwoo LeeKernel & BSP

And the vendors were genuinely collaborative. Laura from Qorvix pointed out the missing validity check, which turned out to be the most important part of the fix. Stefan from Kestrel was open about the firmware change while still defending it on technical grounds. Nobody wasted time protecting their company's reputation.

What Went Poorly

22 · Jiwoo LeeKernel & BSP

Now, what went poorly, apart from the technical causes. The first thing is that it took two days from the first panic in the lab to the triage meeting. The panics appeared on Saturday morning, but the dumps sat in the lab's queue until Monday, because nobody on the weekend rotation knew they were urgent.

23 · Minji SeoSQE

That's fair, and it's on SQE's process. Our weekend rotation was set up for collecting results, not for judging severity. We've since added a simple rule: any kernel panic signature that appears on more than three devices in a stress run pages the kernel on-call engineer immediately, weekend or not.

24 · Grace JungRelease Management

The second thing that went poorly was communication with the carriers. We told them about the delay on the same day as the original release date. Two carriers had already scheduled their store staff training around the update, and they had to cancel at the last minute.

25 · Rachel KimPlatform Framework

Why so late? The hold decision was made on August 3rd, three days before the planned release.

26 · Grace JungRelease Management

Because we wanted to give them a new date at the same time, and we didn't have one until Wednesday. In hindsight, it would have been better to tell them immediately that the release was on hold, and send the new date separately. Partners can plan around uncertainty; they can't plan around silence.

27 · Daniel ChoKernel & BSP

That's a sentence worth writing down. It applies to vendors and internal teams as much as to carriers.

Where We Got Lucky

28 · Jiwoo LeeKernel & BSP

This is my favorite section in any postmortem: where did we get lucky? Luck is a hidden risk, because next time it might not be there.

29 · Minji SeoSQE

We were lucky that the stress lab had capacity that week. If the Orion Fold robots had been running at the time, the Orion candidate would have waited several days for a slot, and the release might have gone out before the stress run finished. Our process didn't guarantee the stress run happened before release; it just happened to.

30 · Grace JungRelease Management

That's uncomfortable to hear, but it's true. Our release checklist says 'stress testing complete', but it doesn't define how long, or what happens if the lab is busy. I've already changed that: a release candidate can't be approved without at least seventy-two hours of stress testing on a hundred devices, period.

31 · Hyunsoo BaeKernel & BSP

We were also lucky that Orion Lite uses a different storage part. The timeout change was in the common kernel branch, so if Orion Lite had used the same firmware, both products would have been affected, and Orion Lite's hotfix was going out the same week.

32 · Daniel ChoKernel & BSP

Two pieces of luck, and both point to the same thing: we depend on timing and coincidence more than we'd like. Good catch, both of you.

Debate: Blameless versus Accountable

33 · Rachel KimPlatform Framework

I want to raise a question that I know some managers ask. If postmortems are blameless, where does accountability come in? If nobody is responsible, won't people become careless?

34 · Daniel ChoKernel & BSP

That's the most common objection, and it's a fair one. My answer is that blameless doesn't mean no accountability. It means accountability for the future, not punishment for the past. Every action item from this postmortem has a named owner and a date. If those don't get done, that's where accountability applies.

35 · Minji SeoSQE

From the testing side, I'd add a practical argument. If people expect blame, they hide information. In the triage meeting, Hyunsoo said 'that's on us' within the first fifteen minutes, which saved us at least a day of investigation. In a blame culture, people would have spent that day defending their changes.

36 · Hyunsoo BaeKernel & BSP

And honestly, I said it because I knew this team wouldn't punish me for it. If I'd been worried about my performance review, I might have spent the morning trying to prove the firmware was the problem.

37 · Grace JungRelease Management

There is one exception I'd mention, though. Blameless assumes good faith. If someone knowingly skips a required test or hides a known problem, that's a different conversation. But in my experience, that's extremely rare, and treating every incident as if it might be that case does far more harm than good.

38 · Rachel KimPlatform Framework

That's a helpful distinction. Good faith mistakes get a blameless review; bad faith is a management issue, handled separately.

Action Items and Their Status

39 · Jiwoo LeeKernel & BSP

Let's review the action items that came out of this incident and where they stand. One: the validity check in the completion path. Done, shipped in the replacement build. Two: the timeout aligned with the specification. Done. Three: any change tagged as storage triggers an overnight storage stress run on fifty devices. Tom set that up last week, and it's already caught one minor regression.

40 · Grace JungRelease Management

Four: vendor firmware updates are treated as changes and trigger the same subsystem tests as code. I proposed it at the release council today, together with the slower staged rollout for security-related firmware. Both were approved, effective September 1st.

41 · Minji SeoSQE

Five: the minimum seventy-two-hour stress requirement for release candidates, which Grace already added to the checklist. That one directly removes the luck we depended on.

42 · Daniel ChoKernel & BSP

That's an impressive completion rate for a postmortem only three weeks after the incident. In my experience, half of postmortem action items are never done. What made the difference here?

43 · Grace JungRelease Management

Mostly that every item had an owner who cared about it, and the items were small enough to finish. We deliberately avoided vague items like 'improve testing'. Every action is specific enough that you can tell whether it's done.

Measuring Whether It Worked

44 · Rachel KimPlatform Framework

How will you know whether the actions actually prevent the next incident, rather than just feeling better?

45 · Jiwoo LeeKernel & BSP

For the overnight storage runs, we're tracking how many issues they catch before a release candidate. One already, in its first week. For the firmware rule, we'll compare how many firmware-related issues are found in the field over the next six months against the last six. If the number doesn't go down, the rule isn't working, and we'll revisit it.

46 · Minji SeoSQE

I'd also schedule a short follow-up review in three months. Not a full postmortem, just thirty minutes to check whether each action is still being followed. Rules that nobody checks tend to fade quietly.

47 · Daniel ChoKernel & BSP

Good. Let's put that follow-up in the calendar today, so it isn't forgotten. I'll also add the postmortem to our quarterly engineering review, so the action items are visible to management and not only to the people in this room.

Running Your Own Postmortem

48 · Rachel KimPlatform Framework

For those of us who want to run postmortems in our own teams, what's the one thing you'd recommend?

49 · Jiwoo LeeKernel & BSP

Write the timeline first, and share it with everyone involved before the meeting. Half of the value comes from people correcting the timeline. In my first draft, I had the firmware integration date wrong by four days, and Hyunsoo caught it. That changed how we understood the sequence.

50 · Hyunsoo BaeKernel & BSP

And invite the people who made the decisions, not just the people who fixed the problem. The fix tells you what was broken. The decisions tell you why it got broken.

51 · Grace JungRelease Management

I'd add: keep the 'where we got lucky' section. It's the part people usually skip, and it's often where the most important improvements come from.

52 · Daniel ChoKernel & BSP

Jiwoo will put the full postmortem document on the wiki, including the timeline and the action items. 고마워요, Daniel. 솔직히 이걸 발표하는 게 조금 긴장됐습니다. 관련된 결정 중 일부를 현수 님과 저를 포함해 이 방에 있는 사람들이 내렸거든요. 하지만 바로 그래서 할 가치가 있다고 생각합니다.

타임라인

08 · Jiwoo LeeKernel & BSP

타임라인부터 시작하겠습니다. 정확한 타임라인이 없는 포스트모템은 기억에 대한 논쟁이 되거든요. 6월 30일, 현수 님이 대기 전력을 아끼려고 스토리지 서스펜드 타임아웃을 500밀리초에서 200밀리초로 줄이는 변경을 머지했습니다. 당시 쓰던 스토리지 펌웨어로 테스트했고, 잘 동작했습니다.

09 · Jiwoo LeeKernel & BSP

7월 7일, 일상적인 벤더 업데이트의 일환으로 다른 엔지니어가 Kestrel의 새 스토리지 펌웨어를 통합했습니다. 더 큰 쓰기 버퍼가 활성화됐죠. 그 펌웨어는 표준 스토리지 기능 테스트로 테스트됐고 통과했습니다. 7월 28일, 두 변경을 모두 포함한 릴리스 후보가 SQE 스트레스 랩에 들어갔습니다. 8월 1일, SQE가 200대에서 패닉 11건을 발견했습니다. 8월 3일, 분류 회의를 열고 릴리스를 보류했습니다.

10 · Jiwoo LeeKernel & BSP

8월 3일부터 10일까지 네 그룹 실험을 돌려 경쟁 상태를 확인하고 수정을 검증했습니다. 8월 11일 대체 빌드를 승인했고, 8월 18일 단계적 배포를 시작했습니다. 발견부터 수정 승인까지 총 10일, 원래 릴리스 대비 총 지연은 12일이었습니다.

11 · Rachel KimPlatform Framework

확인 질문 하나 해도 될까요? 7월 7일부터 7월 28일 사이에 릴리스 후보 전에 두 변경이 어디서든 함께 돌아간 적이 있나요?

12 · Minji SeoSQE

야간 빌드에서는 함께 돌아갔지만, 단말당 약 한 시간 걸리는 짧은 기능 테스트뿐이었습니다. 패닉이 나려면 지속적인 쓰기 트래픽과 화면 꺼짐 이벤트가 겹쳐야 했고, 스트레스를 받는 단말에서도 평균 6시간이 지나야 나타났습니다. 그래서 그걸 볼 만큼 오래 돈 야간 테스트는 없었습니다.

기여 요인

13 · Jiwoo LeeKernel & BSP

이제 기여 요인입니다. 이런 사고에는 단일 근본 원인이 거의 없기 때문에 일부러 복수형으로 '요인들'이라고 합니다. 다섯 가지를 찾았습니다. 첫째, 드라이버에 잠재된 버그가 있었습니다. 완료 경로가 요청이 아직 유효한지 확인하지 않았죠. 그 버그는 최소 2년 동안 존재했습니다.

14 · Jiwoo LeeKernel & BSP

둘째, 타임아웃 변경으로 에러 핸들러가 훨씬 자주 실행돼서 드문 경쟁 상태가 흔한 경쟁 상태가 됐습니다. 셋째, 펌웨어 변경으로 플러시가 더 오래 걸려서 타임아웃이 더욱 자주 발동했습니다. 넷째, 두 변경은 서로 다른 엔지니어가 담당하고 따로 테스트해서, 아무도 그 조합을 테스트하지 않았습니다. 다섯째, 스트레스 테스트가 릴리스 후보에서만 돌아서, 조합이 생긴 지 3주가 지나서야 스트레스 테스트를 받았습니다.

15 · Daniel ChoKernel & BSP

목록에 없는 걸 보세요. '현수 님이 타임아웃을 줄였다'는 없습니다. 그건 사건이지 원인이 아닙니다. 원인은 저희 절차가 타이밍 변경을 한 펌웨어로 검증하고 다른 펌웨어로 출시하는 걸 허용했다는 겁니다.

그렇게 봐 주셔서 감사합니다. 솔직히 변경할 때 신중하게 테스트했습니다. 단말 50대로 플러시 시간을 측정했고 두 배 이상의 여유를 둔 값을 골랐습니다. 문제는 그 여유가 곧 바뀔 펌웨어를 기준으로 했다는 거였고, 저로서는 그걸 알 방법이 없었습니다.

17 · Rachel KimPlatform Framework

저에게 정말 유용한 포인트네요. 비난 중심의 검토였다면 대화는 '타임아웃이 너무 짧았다'에서 멈췄을 겁니다. 여기서는 '변경한 사람이 펌웨어가 바뀐다는 걸 왜 몰랐나'로 이어지고, 그게 다음 사고를 실제로 막는 수정으로 이어지네요.

잘된 점

18 · Jiwoo LeeKernel & BSP

좋은 포스트모템은 잘못된 것만 보지 않습니다. 몇 가지는 아주 잘됐고, 계속 그렇게 해야 합니다. 스트레스 랩이 고객 한 명도 보기 전에 문제를 잡았습니다. 시스템이 설계대로 동작한 거죠.

19 · Minji SeoSQE

네 그룹 실험도 돋보였습니다. 누구의 변경이 원인인지 논쟁하는 대신, 3일 만에 데이터로 질문에 답하는 실험을 설계했습니다. 대조군이 있어서 결과에 이견의 여지가 없었습니다.

20 · Grace JungRelease Management
